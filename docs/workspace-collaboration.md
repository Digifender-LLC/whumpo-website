This is the implementer contract for Business Pro workspace collaboration: owner / editor / viewer / billing roles, explicit project and release approval, invitations, membership, audit and full owner transfer. It reuses the delivered [workspace identity](workspace-identity.md), [feature admission](feature-admission.md), [Chat interaction](chat-interactions.md) and [view](workspace-views.md) foundations rather than adding authentication, entitlement, job or approval engines. It follows [composition architecture](roadmap/composition-architecture.md) and [alignment](roadmap/alignment.md): configure and register once, never build a parallel authority.

## Scope and non-goals

In scope: the role and capability model; the tenant/actor split every converted handler needs; invitation, claim, membership, removal, plan-loss and ownership-transfer lifecycles; workspace closure; approval provenance and stale-review invalidation; job and receipt rules; audit, lifecycle, protection and safety-hold rules; owner Chat/view bindings; verification gates; and child-packet decomposition.

Not in scope and not implied: a public seat count, price, quota or allowance (the owner-set first-release limit of five active members is an internal initial capacity limit, [recorded below](#member-limit), and is none of these); real-money billing or payer changes; new provider setup; native (Android) member clients; staging, portfolio or bulk-change features that consume this contract; invite-as-signup (an invitation never creates an account); a legacy reader, compatibility alias or data-preserving conversion (the owner's [current-version rule](current-version-policy.md) applies: one current schema, a reviewed empty-database cutover, disposable test data). Retention windows are **not approved**; every retention statement below is a proposal under the existing LC decisions in [data lifecycle](data-lifecycle.md).

## Terms, and who is not a member

- **Account**: one person's identity (passkeys, kit, sessions, personal profile and Chat). Defined by [workspace identity](workspace-identity.md). The `users` row is, and stays, an account.
- **Workspace (tenant)**: the unit that owns projects, versions, reviews, provider credentials, entitlements and the payer relationship. It is its own row and is never a person's account.
- **Acting context**: `{tenantId, actorAccountId, membershipId, membershipRevision, principal}`. **Every** actor, including a sole owner, acts through a membership; there is no "owner without membership" form once the tenant is not the account.
- **Member**: an account with an `active` membership row for a workspace. Role: `owner`, `editor`, `viewer` or `billing`. Optional explicit grants (off by default) extend an editor. Owners are members and count toward the [member limit](#member-limit). A pending invitation, or a claim not yet confirmed, is **not** an active membership: no row is `active` until the committing activation batch runs.
- **Member-only account**: a completed account that holds at least one active membership and owns no workspace or project of its own ([below](#member-only-accounts)).
- **Not members and never promoted by this feature**: Creator guest reviewers (accountless expiring [preview links](guest-previews.md)); Business approved notification recipients ([contact notification destinations](contact-forms.md)); customer-application visitors and project staff ([access roles](shared-modules.md)); platform operators and the support/safety case surface; anyone holding only a copied link, role string, receipt or model statement.

## Invariants

1. **One identity, one authority path.** Members sign in with the same passkey/kit/recovery model; there is no member password, magic link, workspace-only login or separate recovery. Membership removal revokes only that workspace's authority and never a personal credential; an emergency personal key removal or recovery advances the account epoch and therefore ends every workspace access through that account at the next admission ([identity authority](identity-authority.md)).
2. **Membership is authority only when evaluated server-side at the effect.** A role, grant, selected workspace, cached view, Chat statement, saved interaction, preview link or receipt ID is not authority. Admission rechecks tenant, membership revision, role, grants, project scope and inclusion inside the committing statements.
3. **Spend, publication, provider writes, membership changes and ownership changes are separate capabilities** that no role holds by accident. An action with several effects needs **all** of them ([effect sets](#roles-grants-and-effect-sets)); the strongest effect never authorizes the others. Editors have none without an explicit grant; ownership and billing-plan authority are not grantable.
4. **Models prepare; named humans confirm.** Typed controls, exact immutable reviews and recent passkey proof for consequential effects are unchanged ([security register](security-risk-actions.md), [tenant authority audit](tenant-authority-audit.md)). Visitors, webhooks, scheduled rules and view rendering never invoke the owner maintainer or borrow member identity.
5. **Immutable history stays immutable.** Reviews, versions, receipts and published content keep the tenant and actor provenance they were created with. Ownership change is a new fact, never an update to history.
6. **Exit paths never depend on the paid feature.** Owner roster read, member removal, voluntary leave, export, disconnect and uncertain-effect readback stay reachable after plan loss ([feature loss table](plan-feature-contract.md#loss-of-eligibility-and-protected-access)). A revoked collaborator is never treated as a protected owner.
7. **Current-version only, honest recovery.** One current schema and API; stale clients and schemas fail explicitly; lost responses are resolved by authenticated readback, never by resending.
8. **Providers' consent is personal and never moves implicitly.** An OAuth authorization belongs to the person who gave it. Ownership change, project move or role change never re-labels, re-seals or reuses it for someone else, and **no ownership operation re-authorizes a provider**: the new owner connects with their own consent ([custody](#custody-consent-and-the-actor-reference)). A provider-wide grant revocation that reaches the person's authorizations outside the workspace is likewise personal consent: only the consenting actor performs it, and no ownership operation runs it implicitly.

## Ownership, transfer and the tenant key

The WH-054 packet requires an owner-transfer lifecycle. This section keeps that requirement intact: **full transfer** means the accountable owner becomes another account while the workspace, its projects, `{projectId}.whumpo.site` URLs, immutable history and uncertain-operation authority are preserved, and the departing owner may then delete their personal account without losing any of it. A co-owner is not a substitute.

### Three different operations

| Operation | What changes | What must not change | Needs a tenant that outlives one person's account? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Co-owner addition** | A second account holds the `owner` role. | Tenant identity, payer, custody, history. | No, but the workspace then lives or dies with whichever account anchors it. |
| **Owner authority transfer** | Accountable owner and payer-of-record become another account; the sender leaves or takes a lesser role. The sender's personal account is untouched and may later be deleted. | Workspace ID, project IDs and URLs, immutable versions/reviews/receipts, published sites, uncertain-operation authority. | **Yes**, unless the sender's login row *is* the tenant row. |
| **Project transfer** | One project moves between two workspaces. | Project ID and URL, immutable history and its origin provenance. | Yes. **Not designed here** ([below](#project-transfer)). |

| --- | --- |

| Payer / liability | `stripe_customers`, `stripe_subscription_orders`, `stripe_subscriptions`, `stripe_paid_invoices` are keyed by `user_id`; the confirm guard reads the plan of `NEW.user_id`. |

### Why in-place re-keying is refused (not "impossible")

Option B would update tenant columns that dozens of triggers make immutable, reseal provider tokens under a new context and move a payer record, all while claiming immutable reviews are unchanged. Each step is technically possible; together they defeat the invariants that make reviews trustworthy, and resealing a token transfers one person's provider consent to another. **B is refused by the project's own invariants, not by technical impossibility.** The statement "transfer cannot be done without an organization rewrite" is not proven either: it is true only if the tenant key must be the departing person's row (A) or change in place (B).

### Engineering decision: C2, a dedicated `workspaces` tenant (proposed; engineering, not an owner question)

The first draft recommended C1 on least-DDL grounds. That argument does not survive the project's own rules, and C1's cost was understated:

1. **The DDL saving is not real here.** The platform has one current schema and a reviewed empty-database cutover; there is no data to preserve, so rewriting canonical inputs and triggers costs source churn, not migration risk. Meanwhile **source and test conversion is required under C1 as well** ([below](#converting-every-workspace-owner-path)): the tenant is no longer the account, so every owner path changes regardless. C1 saves only the column renames, and only by giving up typing.
2. **C1 overloads a person and a tenant across 111 columns.** One foreign-key type for both means correctness depends on a guard trigger on every tenant column and on every handler choosing the right ID forever. The failure mode is silent mis-tenanting (an account ID written as a tenant, or a workspace ID read as a person), which is exactly the tenant/actor confusion this contract exists to prevent. C2 turns most of that class into a foreign-key or compile-time failure.
3. **C2 makes the deletion invariants structural.** A person's account deletion cannot cascade into a workspace because no such reference exists; the workspace-closure path ([below](#workspace-closure-and-personal-account-deletion)) is therefore the only way a workspace is removed, and it is explicit.
4. **Honest costs of C2.** Tenant-scoped tables (the 111 `users`-referencing tables minus the account-scoped ones; the 78 indirect tables derive their tenant through a project or another table's key and need review) change key and triggers, including the 57 triggers that repeat `owner_user_id = NEW.user_id` and 62 that enforce immutability of the tenant column (both re-counted October 8 by text pattern); Stripe, vault, entitlement-coverage and budget tables move to the workspace; sealing contexts are rebuilt on the workspace ID plus the authorizing actor. **Partly measured, not proven:** an in-memory spike at `b319f33` retargeted 97 tenant-class foreign keys and exercised the person-deletion, last-owner and transfer invariants (19 of 22 checks passed), but it was a mechanical key rename, not an implementation, and its three failures are findings the schema child must design around ([below](#what-the-feasibility-spike-showed)). Handler conversion, closure purge, custody resealing and billing were not exercised, so total effort stays **unmeasured**. The schema child still starts with the classification registry and a composed in-memory build under the new keys, and its exit gate includes the unchanged owner suites ([verification](#verification-and-acceptance-gates)). If C2 cannot be completed within one reviewed cutover, the fallback is C1 **with** a kind guard on every tenant column, not option A.

Shape (conceptual; names follow repository conventions at implementation):

- `users` stays an account (`id`, `email`, `created_at`) and `user_quota` counts accounts only. A new `workspaces` row holds the tenant: ID, state (`active`, `closing`, `closed-held`), an **ownership epoch** and a revision. It has no owner column and no email.
- **Ownership is only memberships.** `workspace_memberships` rows carry workspace, account, role, grants, scope, state, revision, a pseudonymous per-(workspace, account) `actor_ref`, and the collaboration epoch ([durable suspension](#plan-eligibility-downgrade-and-durable-suspension)). A database guard keeps every `active` workspace at ≥ 1 active owner.
- Tenant columns are named `workspace_id` and reference `workspaces`; account columns keep `user_id`; tables that need both (reviews, receipts, Chat) carry `workspace_id` plus actor provenance columns. Immutability triggers move to `workspace_id` and are unchanged in effect.
- **Creation.** A person's first workspace and owner membership are created in one transaction after identity completion, at the first-project welcome, so deployed identity ceremonies stay untouched. Creating a workspace is **not** a precondition of using an invited one: a completed account with an active membership and no workspace of its own is a valid, supported state (owner decision, [member-only accounts](#member-only-accounts)).

### What the feasibility spike showed

A read-only review of `b319f33` built the composed schema in memory, retargeted the 97 tenant-class foreign keys to a new `workspaces` table, ran `RENAME COLUMN … TO workspace_id` (SQLite re-parses every trigger and view), added nullable actor columns, and added `workspaces`, `workspace_actors` and `workspace_memberships` with last-owner and account-deletion guards. **This was a mechanical key rename, not an implementation**; it passed 19 of 22 checks (person ID rejected as tenant; deleting an editor's account leaves workspace, project and history intact with the actor reference surviving; a sole active owner cannot delete their account; the last owner cannot leave; promote-then-leave then deletion leaves the workspace intact; the identity credential chain stays on the account). Its three failures are **prerequisites for the schema child, not things that creating the schema will catch**:

1. **Three renames fail** because trigger and view bodies reach the column through aliases SQLite cannot follow: trigger `cms_publication_media_admit` (`a.user_id`), view `service_client_valid_proposals` (`c.owner_user_id`) and trigger `publication_confirm_guard` (`user_id` through the `plan_entitlements` view). A rename script cannot finish the job.
2. **At least 22 trigger bodies stay valid SQL and become silently wrong.** 19 retention guards of the form `EXISTS(SELECT 1 FROM users WHERE id = OLD.workspace_id)` can never match, so tenant receipts become deletable; 3 hold triggers (`safety_hold_chat_draft`, `safety_hold_guest_feedback_retained`, `safety_hold_guest_link_retained`) compare a person's `subject_user_id` with a workspace column. This is a lower bound: views and triggers on the three columns that failed to rename (for example `safety_held_projects` and `operator_contained_projects`, `p.owner_user_id = c.subject_id`) are equally wrong and were not counted.
3. **An account hold stops protecting the project.** In the current schema an account-scope preservation hold on the owner blocks `DELETE projects` (`safety_preservation_hold`); after the mechanical retarget the same delete succeeded.

SQLite accepts `CREATE TRIGGER` bodies that name unknown columns and fails only at the first DML that fires them, so the current-schema artifact check (execute plus `foreign_key_check`) **cannot** detect stale bodies. The first draft's claim that stale SQL "fails loudly" is therefore withdrawn. Not exercised: closure purge, custody resealing, billing, the lease, any handler, and NOT NULL actor columns (these need rebuilt `CREATE` statements, that is, canonical-input edits).

### Coordinated current-schema consequences

All of these are done in one reviewed empty-database cutover under the [release rules](releases.md); there is no conversion of existing test accounts.

- **Classification gate.** Every `users`-referencing column is classified exactly once. The feasibility review proposed a classification for all 111 columns (proposed, not frozen): **7 `account`** (identity, profile, eligibility, signup invitation, funnel, profile-photo safety, welcome onboarding; these keep `REFERENCES users`), **16 `workspace`**, **65 `workspace+actor`** (tenant plus a pseudonymous actor reference: reviews, applies, receipts, Chat, jobs, uploads), **12 `workspace+custody`** (provider vault, OAuth, revocation, rotation, backups), **4 `workspace+payer`** (Stripe customer, order, subscription, invoice), **1 `polymorphic`** (`photo_scan_tickets`) and **6 `unknown`** (`workspace_state`, `workspace_preferences`, `workspace_layout_receipts`, `content_screening_decisions`, `content_risk_evidence`, `content_risk_reversals`). **Those are the review's historical counts and are not recomputed here.** A later source-only follow-up classified the seven `unknown`/`polymorphic` entries and refined the two entries counted as `workspace+custody` (`github_connections`) and `workspace+actor` (`backend_nominations`); see [Source-classified dispositions](#source-classified-dispositions-for-the-seven-undecided-entries-and-two-refinements). Those dispositions are engineering recommendations derived from current semantics, not owner choices, and they are recorded as `proposed` entries in the packet 1a registry ([`docs/tenant-classification.json`](tenant-classification.json)) with source-reading evidence only: the typed-subject and split entries are counted once against their current column and list their intended outputs beside it, so the registry's totals (7 `account`, 17 `workspace`, 68 `workspace+actor`, 12 `workspace+custody`, 4 `workspace+payer`, 2 typed-subject, 1 split; 105 `proposed`, 6 `undecided`) differ from the review's historical counts above. The registry check (`tools/tenant-classification.mjs`, beside `tools/lifecycle-catalog.mjs` and `tools/safety-hold-inventory.mjs`) fails on any unclassified or stale column and has a negative control with an injected new `users` foreign key. **Non-foreign-key identity columns are classified too**, because a mechanical rename must not touch them: hold and containment subjects (`subject_user_id` in four tables, `operator_containments.subject_id`), `file_cleanup.owner_user_id`, `domain_reconciliations.user_id`, the textual `confirmed_by` columns (four tables), `module_request_receipts.principal_hash`, provider `github_user_id`, and the Cloudflare `account_id` columns, which identify provider accounts, not persons.
- **Roots and closure: ordered and journaled, not a cascade.** `projects` and every tenant root reference `workspaces`; identity tables reference accounts. The first draft's "tenant tables cascade from `workspaces`" is **withdrawn**. 24 tenant columns are `NO ACTION`/`RESTRICT` on purpose (application reviews and recovery points, backend and CMS reviews, `chat_review_receipts`, spend attempts and buckets, domain connections, OAuth exchanges, rotation guards, revocations, refresh custody), and five `app_*` tables (`app_spaces`, `app_migration_checks`, `app_migration_approvals`, `app_recovery_points`, `app_restore_reviews`) form a foreign-key cycle. The spike confirmed `DELETE workspaces` is blocked by these rows. A workspace is therefore removed only by the journaled, resumable [closure](#workspace-closure-and-personal-account-deletion), which purges tenant tables in an explicit order that breaks the cycle and the restrict chain and deletes the `workspaces` row last. Account deletion never reaches a workspace row because no key points from a workspace to a person.
- **Triggers and views are rebuilt explicitly, then gated.** The canonical inputs rewrite every trigger and view that touches a re-keyed column by hand; no rename script produces them. The result is held to these gates, which exist because creating the schema succeeds regardless:
  1. A **re-parse validator** (for example `ALTER TABLE … RENAME` round-trips over every trigger and view) plus a **behavior test per rewritten trigger** that executes its DML in a fixture on both the allowing and the aborting branch.
  2. A **cross-type lint** that fails any trigger or view comparing a `workspace_id` with `users.id`, `subject_user_id`, `subject_id` or an `account`-class column.
  3. The 26 "retained while the account exists" guards (`FROM users WHERE id = OLD.…`) re-keyed per class: tenant rows are retained while the **workspace** exists; actor rows are never tied to a person's existence.
  4. **Hold and no-op regressions**: for each hold and containment subject kind, a test proves that a placed hold still blocks the deletions and ownership changes it is meant to block. A test that passes because a trigger can no longer match anything is a failure, and the spike's demonstration (a hold that stopped protecting the project) is the regression to reproduce first.
- **Billing.** Stripe customer, order, subscription and invoice rows and entitlement coverage attach to the workspace; the account that completes checkout is recorded as **payer-of-record provenance** (a stable actor reference, see below). See *Payer on transfer*. **The billing role never receives the payer's hosted invoice link.** `stripe_paid_invoices.hosted_url` is Stripe's hosted invoice page, which the [API reference](https://docs.stripe.com/api/invoices/object) (read October 8, 2026) describes as allowing customers to view and pay an invoice; Whumpo treats possession of that URL as access to the payer's personal billing details. The billing role is given intervals, amounts and usage only. The URL (today returned in the owner's invoice list, [`src/stripe-billing.ts:116`](../src/stripe-billing.ts)) is restricted to the payer-of-record actor under the C2 split of that list (proposed; a narrower or wider audience is a product call).
- **Per-workspace multiplication must be bounded before multi-workspace activation.** The project quota (20 projects per account, trigger `project_quota`), `inference_budgets(user_id, period)` and the Explore owner-testing ceiling ($20 per account per month, [pricing policy](pricing-and-model-policy.md)) become per-workspace under the new key. If one account may create N workspaces, N times the platform-funded budget and project quota becomes reachable, and ownership transfer or co-owner addition can reach the same multiplication through a person who then creates a further workspace. **Two things are kept apart here.** The *current baseline* is one tenant per account, because the account is the tenant today. The *future policy* (how many workspaces one account may own or fund, and by what mechanism) is **not approved, not designed and not capped by this document**: no numeric limit and no temporary product limit is adopted. This contract specifies only the creation of a person's first workspace ([Shape](#engineering-decision-c2-a-dedicated-workspaces-tenant-proposed-engineering-not-an-owner-question)), which includes the first workspace of an account that began as member-only ([below](#member-only-accounts)); that follows the ordinary creation and entitlement rules and is not restricted here. A path by which one account owns or funds a further workspace (a second creation, a transfer to an account that already owns one, or a co-owner addition that leads to one) is multi-workspace activation and must not ship until the owner decides the bound. The proposed options, for the owner to choose between or replace, are in [question 4](#remaining-product-questions).
- **Lifecycle, closure inventory, export, restore suppression** register in the same change ([Audit and lifecycle](#audit-lifecycle-protection-export-and-restore)).
- **Collisions.** Open pull requests change the same files ([Sequencing](#sequencing-with-unmerged-work)); reconcile before any code starts.

**Chat head, layout preferences and layout receipts (`workspace+actor`, actor-private).**
- *Why not workspace-level:* the head is the compare-and-set counter for `chat_turns`, and the single-flight rule (no second `pending` turn) is evaluated per key; a workspace-level head would serialize or collide every member. Chat is already private to the acting account within a workspace ([Roles](#roles-grants-and-effect-sets)). The layout receipt is welded to a private Chat turn by trigger, a shared layout would need one revision counter across editors, and it would publish user-chosen tab labels and project IDs outside a member's scope. A per-account layout is wrong too, because project IDs mean something only inside one workspace. A *shared or team layout* would be a new feature, is not part of WH-054 and is not designed here.
- *Keys and triggers:* preferences PK `(workspace_id, actor_ref)`; receipts PK `(workspace_id, actor_ref, request_id)` with `UNIQUE(workspace_id, actor_ref, to_revision)` and the existing `UNIQUE(origin_turn_id)`; the `origin_turn_id` trigger compares `(workspace_id, actor_ref)` with `chat_turns`; project checks become `workspace_id` plus the actor's scope; preferences gain an explicit actor-column immutability trigger (the receipts' blanket update refusal already covers theirs). A sole owner behaves exactly as today. The welcome trigger (`schema/platform.sql` around line 4292) must create the workspace, actor, owner membership and head in one transaction.
- *Removal candidates, not silent drops:* `step`, `idea`, `brief_project_id` and `brief_stage` have no reader in `src`, `tools` or `schema` other than resets to `''`/`NULL` (`src/site-agent.ts:275`, the welcome trigger) and four `step='ready'` guards that mean "the head exists". They are **removal candidates**: the canonical-input review must re-confirm that no reader exists before dropping them, replace the guards with head existence, and, if any is kept, scope-check `brief_project_id` (a textual project reference with no foreign key).
- *Audit and holds:* the receipts are immutable presentation history for undo and replay, not authority audit, and must not be copied into the workspace audit log; their immutability lasts for the life of the actor-private row. Neither table has an independent retention or preservation need: both follow the private-Chat disposition of `chat_turns` and the `safety_hold_chat_draft` rule, so an account hold on the actor's person or a workspace hold blocks their purge, and workspace closure purges them in order after the hold check. They join the hold inventory as covered by the actor's private-data purge.

**Content screening decisions (`workspace`, sticky across actors).**
- The row is a requirement about the *digest of artifact content*, not about a person. If the key included `actor_ref`, a second member submitting identical content would receive a fresh, unsampled decision and bypass a sticky requirement. Provenance of who prepared the artifact already lives on the `workspace+actor` review or version row that carries the digest.
- The sampling decision is an HMAC over a message that includes `target.userId` ([`src/content-screening.ts:36`](../src/content-screening.ts)); the proposal replaces it with `workspace_id`, so retries by different members do not reroll and an ownership change does not reroll. The capacity triggers (10,000 per owner, 100,000 global) become per workspace.
- Every reader must be **hand-rewritten**, not renamed: the three source gates ([`src/project-specification/store.ts`](../src/project-specification/store.ts), `service-clients/owned.ts` and `service-clients/store.ts`), the four views (`project_spec_held`, `service_client_held`, `service_client_valid_proposals` and a second service-client view keyed on `a.user_id`) and the `specification_held` triggers. A gate that keeps comparing `d.user_id = p.owner_user_id` after one side is re-keyed **never matches, and held content then publishes fail-open**; the feasibility spike already failed to rename `service_client_valid_proposals`.
- Existing immutability stays (first reading never changes; escalation only). Only system escalation changes a row, the transfer batch must not touch these rows, and no person-deletion path may reach them (no foreign key to a person). A workspace hold covers them; an account hold on the preparer does not (no silent expansion). `reserveContentScreening` has no runtime caller today, only tests, so the conversion is a schema and gate change, not a behavior change.

**Content risk evidence and reversals (typed subject).**
- The ledger is operator-written, immutable and never stores content (`evidence_ref` and `reviewer_ref` are opaque). Case evidence is admitted only when the evidence subject equals the case subject (`safety_case_evidence_admit`), and the contract now has two hold and case subject kinds ([Safety holds](#safety-holds)), so an account-keyed row could not attach to a workspace-subject case.
- Fields: `subject_kind IN ('account','workspace')`, `account_id → users ON DELETE CASCADE`, `workspace_id → workspaces`, and a `CHECK` that exactly one is set and matches the kind. The registry lists the two columns separately (`account_id` → `account`, `workspace_id` → `workspace`) and requires the exclusive-or check. **Account kind** keeps today's meaning, person-conduct findings that cascade with the account unless a hold applies; it is deliberately **not** attached to `workspace_actors`, whose `SET NULL` pseudonymization would turn a safety ledger into a retained archive past account deletion, which [content safety](content-safety.md) says it is not. **Workspace kind** holds findings about workspace content not attributable to a person, purged only by closure and kept under `closed-held`.
- Reversals carry the same two columns with the subject of the evidence row they reverse (two partial unique indexes replace `PRIMARY KEY(user_id, incident_id)`; `risk_incident_missing` matches on kind and id). The two "retained while the subject exists" guards are rewritten per kind (`FROM users` for account, `FROM workspaces` for workspace); a guard written as `EXISTS (… FROM users WHERE id = OLD.workspace_id)` never matches. `readContentRiskHistory` and the operator review input take `{subjectKind, subjectId}` and the reviewed digest covers the kind. Operator provenance stays the opaque `reviewer_ref`; no `actor_ref` is added.
- A WH-133 activation-time item, not a schema blocker: scanning a member-prepared artifact will need the preparer's account history combined with the workspace history. The planner is inactive and has no runtime caller at the baseline. This remains an input to the existing WH-133/LC-06 review and adds no gate.

**Photo scan tickets (split).** The ticket is a transient scanner attempt (a 15-minute reservation with a bearer `authority_hash`) and records no human actor; it has no runtime caller at the baseline, only tests. A `kind` column that is sometimes a person's profile and sometimes a project or CMS upload hides a foreign-key type difference the registry gate can never classify as one column, so the proposal is two tables: `profile_photo_scan_tickets` (`account_id → users ON DELETE CASCADE`, follows `profile_photo_safety`) and `content_scan_tickets` (`workspace_id`, `kind IN ('project','cms')`, no actor, the ticket-owner trigger joins `projects.workspace_id`). The one-reserved-per-subject unique index stays valid because its key does not include the tenant, and the `targets` map in `src/photo-scan-results.ts` also names the ticket table per kind. A single table with two nullable owner columns and a kind check is an acceptable alternative with the same registry and inventory attention. Both tables are registered in `tools/safety-hold-inventory.mjs` (neither is a preservation store), pending `reserved` tickets are reported by the account and workspace closure inventories respectively, and the update-immutability trigger stays.

**GitHub connections (`workspace+custody`, one active slot per workspace).**
- Today one row exists per account (`github_connections` PK `user_id`, `github_oauth_states` PK `user_id`); a new connect retires the old token, and all backups use the single active token. Observable one-slot semantics are preserved by keying `workspace_id`, with an immutable `authorizing_actor_ref` and the provider `github_user_id`. No owner decision or effect set describes concurrent authorizers or a connection-selection UI, so a per-(workspace, authorizer) table is a strict superset that can come later with its own cutover. `github_oauth_states` becomes `(workspace_id, actor_ref)`.
- A reconnect by a different actor holding `manage-connections` replaces the slot; the previous token is retired through the existing revocation path, so the displaced actor's consent ends and is never reused. `github_backups` binds `(workspace_id, authorizing_actor_ref, token_id, github_user_id)` at review time, so a backup prepared under actor A fails closed if the slot changes to B. The sealing context is `whumpo:github:token:{workspace_id}:{authorizing_actor_ref}:{id}` ([Custody](#custody-consent-and-the-actor-reference)). The connected GitHub login is visible only to the authorizing actor and to owners or `manage-connections` holders; other roles see "connected".
- **Two different revocations.** Revoking *a token* ends one workspace's slot. The **app-wide grant revocation** (`src/github-grant-revocation.ts`, one pending per OAuth client platform-wide) deletes the person's whole grant for that client, reaches their other authorizations outside this workspace, and is a **personal-consent effect**: only the consenting actor may prepare and confirm it, and its review row becomes workspace plus authorizing actor. Ownership transfer, member removal and closure use the token-level path and **never** the app-wide route, and never revoke other workspaces' connections implicitly. Revocation receipts stay non-deletable; an unknown outcome uses the existing uncertain state with its reconciliation authority intact, and the receipt carries the transfer-obligation reference.

**Backend nominations (`workspace+actor`, owner-authority consent).** A nomination is permission for Whumpo maintainers to review one exact saved backend version for possible generic reuse; it is not a license or publication and is not a provider credential. It is owner-authority consent about the workspace's content, given by a named person while owner, so it is tenant-bound but person-attributed. Today `backend_nomination_update` proves consent through `p.owner_user_id = NEW.user_id`. Under C2 the tenant does not change at transfer, so a mechanical rename to `workspace_id` would leave the sender's `nominated` row valid under the new owner, converting the former actor's consent into the new owner's. The proposal adds `consent_actor_ref` and `consent_epoch` (set once, immutable, added to the existing immutability trigger) and defines the effective-consent predicate used by operator intake and any future reader: `state='nominated'` ∧ workspace `active` ∧ `consent_epoch = workspaces.ownership_epoch` ∧ the consenting actor still holds an active `owner` membership. Old rows become ineffective without a write; alternatively the transfer batch withdraws them with the transfer ID. Either way the new owner runs a fresh review and gives their own consent. Owner-only and not grantable; any current owner may withdraw (terminal). Accepted generic artifacts follow the `shared-reuse` lifecycle text and are independent of private tenant source.

**What stays unchanged.** Owned-actor privacy and scope checks, immutable audit and receipt rows, and hold and closure protections are retained for every entry above; nothing here widens or narrows what a sole owner can do. **Open, not newly decided:** whether the provider `login` in terminal GitHub revocation receipts is redacted once the authorizing account is deleted is part of the already-open retention question ([question 2](#remaining-product-questions)); the proposal is to keep `github_user_id` (needed for reconciliation) and redact `login`, and actor-row retention is not approved. A team-visible layout is not asked.

**Additional no-op regressions required** (they extend the [hold and no-op regressions](#coordinated-current-schema-consequences); each must fail first against a mechanical rename): a `required=1` screening decision blocks website, service-client and activation actions after conversion **and after an ownership transfer**; identical content from a second member does not receive a fresh decision; account-kind and workspace-kind retention guards each refuse deletion while their own subject exists; a nomination consented to by the sender is not effective for the new owner; a transfer revokes the sender-authorized GitHub slot at token level, does not call the app-wide route, and leaves other workspaces' connections untouched; two members of one workspace keep independent Chat heads and layouts and neither collides with nor sees the other's.

### Actor references, Chat identity and receipt principals

- **A stable pseudonymous actor.** `workspace_actors` holds one row per (workspace, person): an opaque `actor_ref` (never derived from an account ID or email) and a person link, `account_id → users ON DELETE SET NULL`. Memberships reference the actor row, not `users`, and **no history row references `users` for provenance**. Spike-verified: deleting an editor's account leaves the workspace, project and history in place and the `actor_ref` survives with its account link nulled. Re-inviting the same person while the link exists reuses their actor row; after the link is nulled it is never re-linked (nothing remains to link by), so a deleted person is not re-identifiable from the workspace. Pseudonymization or purge of actor rows is a retention question and is not approved.
- **Explicit actor immutability.** Every actor column on a history row (preparer, confirmer, authorizer, payer) gets its own immutability trigger in the canonical inputs. The existing triggers protect the tenant column only; the one actor-column refusal seen in the spike came from `publication_actions`' pending-to-applied rule, not from a general rule, and cannot be relied on elsewhere.
- **Chat composite identity pins the actor.** Chat's composite keys carry the person today (`(id, user_id)`). Under C2 they pin tenant **and** actor, `(id, workspace_id, actor_ref)`, so a submission in a shared workspace cannot reference another member's private interaction. Four Chat composite foreign keys change (`chat_interaction_submissions` to `chat_interactions` and to `chat_interaction_requests`, `chat_interaction_tasks` to `chat_conversations`, `chat_interactions` to `chat_interaction_tasks`); `inference_calls` to `inference_budgets(user_id, period)` is a fifth composite key but a tenant key, `(workspace_id, period)`.
- **Module receipt principal.** `module_request_receipts.principal_hash` is built from `['owner', userId, projectId]` ([`src/shared-modules/api.ts:30`](../src/shared-modules/api.ts)). It must include the workspace **and the acting member's actor reference**; with the workspace alone, two members would share idempotency, replay and receipts.

### Custody, consent and the actor reference

Five encrypted stores seal secrets under a context that embeds the person: `github_refresh_custody`, `github_token_vault`, `cloudflare_token_vault`, `github_oauth_states` and `cloudflare_oauth_states` (the PKCE verifier), as `whumpo:{provider}:{purpose}:{user}:{id}` ([`connection-rotation.ts:5–11,70`](../src/connection-rotation.ts), [`github-connection.ts:22`](../src/github-connection.ts), [`cloudflare-connection.ts:15`](../src/cloudflare-connection.ts), [`github-grant-revocation.ts:42`](../src/github-grant-revocation.ts)). Under C2 the context becomes `whumpo:{provider}:{purpose}:{workspace_id}:{authorizing_actor_ref}:{id}`, using the **stable actor reference** rather than `users.id`, so a pending revocation can still unseal after the authorizing person's account is deleted. All five stores, `connection_rotation_guards.owner_id` and the key-rotation tool change in the same cutover; a store left on the old context is undecryptable, not mis-readable. `github_user_id` stays on the authorizer's record as provider identity.

- **Consent stays personal.** The authorizing actor is recorded as who consented; the workspace is the tenant the connection serves. The sealed credential is never re-sealed or relabeled to another actor.
- **Ownership transfer does not re-authorize a provider.** After commit ([transfer step 4](#owner-authority-transfer-lifecycle)) it revokes each sender-authorized connection **at token level**, one named connection and token at a time, through the existing revocation path; the new owner connects with their own consent. This contract promises no automatic reauthorization and must not describe connections as moving with the workspace.
- **Token-level revocation is not grant-wide revocation.** GitHub's app-wide grant revocation deletes the person's whole grant for the OAuth client and reaches their authorizations in other workspaces and outside Whumpo ([`src/github-grant-revocation.ts`](../src/github-grant-revocation.ts) already discloses this). It is a personal-consent effect that **only the consenting actor may prepare and confirm**. A transfer, removal or closure never runs it and never revokes another workspace's connection implicitly; if the sender wants the wider revocation they perform it themselves.
- **A member's removal or account deletion** revokes or disconnects the connections they authorized ([invalidation matrix](#invalidation-matrix)) before the membership is tombstoned.
- **Cardinality (proposed, engineering).** `github_connections` is keyed by `user_id` today, one active connection per account. Under C2 the proposal is **one active connection per workspace** with an immutable `authorizing_actor_ref`, which preserves the observable one-slot behavior; a per-(workspace, authorizing actor) table would be a strict superset for a later cutover. A transfer then revokes the slot if the sender authorized it. Detail and the rest of the source-classified entries are in [dispositions](#source-classified-dispositions-for-the-seven-undecided-entries-and-two-refinements); the schema child accepts or changes this.

### Converting every workspace-owner path

Under C2 the old rule "the account is the tenant" is never true, so the pre-existing owner behavior cannot be preserved by leaving `admitWorkspace`/`workspacePredicate` as they are: they would deny every owner. The contract is therefore **convert all of them, in the same cutover, and let anything unconverted fail rather than guess**.

1. **One acting-context admission replaces the account-equals-tenant helpers.** `admitWorkspace(db, principal)` stays an *account-level* identity admission. A new tenant admission takes the principal, an explicit `tenantId` and the action's [effect set](#roles-grants-and-effect-sets) and returns a scoped database. The old tenant-taking helpers are **removed**, not aliased: `assertWorkspace(db, userId)`, `workspacePredicate`, `requireFreshWorkspace`, `workspaceTransport`, `workspaceBindings`, `workspacePrincipal`, and the feature-admission family built on them: **`assertFeature`, `featurePredicate`, `requireFeature` and `connectionInclusion`**. `featureRequest` becomes `{workspaceId, …}`, and `requireFeatureScope` and `inclusionPredicate` stop embedding `projects.owner_user_id = ?`. A signature that accepts an account ID as a tenant must not exist. Every call counted in the [source baseline](#source-baseline-what-the-code-does-at-ca17c6a), every `user.id` that reaches tenant SQL, and every maintenance or background entry that reads tenant data is converted.
2. **Conversion does not widen anyone.** A converted former owner path is registered `owner`-only until a later child adds roles to it. Conversion changes the key, not who may act.
3. **Layered conversion assurance; no layer is sufficient alone.** Nothing here relies on stale SQL failing, because it does not ([above](#what-the-feasibility-spike-showed)).
   1. **Typed scoped database.** The tenant admission returns an object that carries the tenant, and the tenant-taking signatures above are removed, so an unconverted call fails `pnpm check`.
   2. **AST entry registry with a dynamic allowlist.** A compiler-based test (the method of the open [boundary audit](#sequencing-with-unmerged-work); not text search) enumerates routes, Chat actions, bindings, job runners, maintenance entries and `tools/` operator scripts, and fails on any that reaches tenant data without an acting context or an explicit platform-obligation actor. It cannot resolve dynamic or composed SQL, so each dynamic site is an **explicit allowlist entry with a reviewed reason**: the 17 statements that interpolate a table name and the 23 that pass a non-literal (counts at the baseline). It also cannot see trigger-side writes (312 triggers).
   3. **A test-time SQLite authorizer oracle.** Node 24's `DatabaseSync.setAuthorizer` reports every table and column read or written at prepare time, including trigger-originated access tagged with the trigger name (verified in the review). The hook point is `tests/sqlite-d1.mjs`, the default path of `tests/helpers/current-database.mjs` (177 tests use `databaseFixture`). Under a tenant context it asserts that every statement touching a tenant table references `workspace_id`. **Limits:** it covers only executed paths; it sees column references, not predicates or bound values; and it is unavailable in the `--d1` (miniflare) mode, where D1 prepared statements are opaque. It first runs report-only.
   4. **Two-workspace canary fixtures.** Value-level isolation is proven by data, not by references: seed workspace B with unique markers and assert none appear under workspace A through any handler, binding, job or export.
4. **Tests preserve behavior, not predicate equality.** The existing single-owner suites (feature admission, workspace admission, publications, identity HTTP, shared modules, guest previews, account closure and others) keep their assertions on status codes, rows, receipts and ordering. Only the fixture seeding changes (create account, workspace and owner membership). No test may assert that an account ID equals a tenant ID. The conversion PR lists the test inventory before and after and every changed assertion with its reason; a weakened assertion is a review blocker. The same suites then run with non-owner contexts and must deny exactly what is not registered.
5. **No partial state ships.** The new keys are a schema change, so the automatic deploy refuses it and merge waits for an owner-authorized cutover; the conversion lands complete or not at all. It is large (the dominant cost of child 1) and may be built as a series of reviews on an integration branch, but only a fully converted tree merges.

### Owner-authority transfer lifecycle

States are `proposed → accepted → completed`, with `cancelled`, `expired` and `refused` as terminal alternatives. There is no persisted `committing` state: commit is a single guarded batch with no external call inside it.

1. **Propose** (current owner, fresh passkey, exact review). Fields: workspace, recipient (must already be an *active member* of this workspace), what the sender becomes (`leave`, `viewer`, `editor` or `billing`; **not** `owner`), a digest of the plain-language liability and custody statement shown, and an expiry (proposed seven days, no extension). At most one open transfer per workspace.
2. **Accept** (recipient, fresh passkey). The recipient acknowledges payer and custody responsibilities by digest. Acceptance changes nothing yet.
3. **Complete** (sender still owner, recipient still active, same ownership epoch and membership revisions, workspace `active`, **no blocking hold** ([holds](#safety-holds)), `pro.members` inclusion not required for the move itself): one batch (a) promotes the recipient to owner, (b) applies the sender's new role or removal, (c) advances the ownership epoch, (d) voids pending reviews, invitations and member-prepared jobs, (e) records unique completion and audit rows, (f) records post-commit obligations. D1 batches are sequential and roll back together ([D1 batch behavior](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/worker-api/d1-database/)); external effects are never inside it.
4. **Post-commit obligations** run as separate receipts under a platform-obligation actor that survives the sender's session: revoke each sender-authorized provider connection **at token level** through the existing revocation path (the recipient must give their own consent; nothing is re-sealed, relabeled or reused), stop sender-authorized backups, pause rules the sender approved until the new owner re-affirms them, and treat the sender's `nominated` backend nominations as no longer effective ([dispositions](#source-classified-dispositions-for-the-seven-undecided-entries-and-two-refinements)). **Grant-wide provider revocation** (GitHub's app-wide grant) is a personal-consent effect that only the consenting actor performs and is **never** an implicit transfer step; no other workspace's connection is revoked. An unknown revocation outcome stays visible in the existing uncertain state and keeps its reconciliation authority and readback; nothing is re-sent, and recovery text must keep saying what is and is not known.
5. **Recovery.** A lost reply is resolved by authenticated readback of the transfer ID by either party. Retried acceptance or completion with the same request identity returns the stored result; a changed payload is `409 request_conflict`. A stale epoch, revision or non-active state is `409` and needs a new proposal. The sender can cancel before completion; after completion there is no undo, only a new transfer.
6. **After completion the sender may delete their personal account** subject only to the closure rules for what they still hold ([below](#workspace-closure-and-personal-account-deletion)); the workspace, projects, URLs and history are unaffected.

**Payer on transfer (unresolved commercial detail, see [questions](#remaining-product-questions)).** Completion changes *who may act*, not who is charged. Whumpo must not silently charge a new person or move a payment method. Proposed default: the already-paid interval continues; the recipient starts their own subscription in their own right (sandbox flow only until real-money activation); the previous payer keeps cancel-at-period-end until the interval ends; with no replacement coverage at expiry, ordinary downgrade rules apply. Proration, refund or card transfer are outside this contract.

### Co-owner addition

Optional, explicit, and never described as a transfer. Only an existing owner can add an owner, by invitation ([owner-only role](#invitations-and-membership-lifecycle)); the recipient accepts with a fresh passkey; any owner can remove a non-owner; removing or demoting an owner requires that owner's own action unless a documented owner-recovery decision is made (unresolved). The last-owner rule applies. Co-owners keep owner authority regardless of plan; *adding* one requires current `pro.members` inclusion.

### Project transfer

**Not designed and not promised.** It is deferred; this section only fixes the constraints the schema must obey so a future design is not precluded and so history cannot be silently reattributed. Today `projects.owner_user_id` is mutable, and children repeat `owner_user_id = NEW.user_id` as a *creation-time* check, so a naive move would make old immutable receipts appear to belong to the destination.

1. **Origin provenance is immutable.** Every review, version, receipt and audit row keeps its own immutable `workspace_id`. Nothing reads `projects.workspace_id` to attribute a historical row; a test fails any authorization, export, billing or audit query that joins a historical row to its project's *current* workspace to find its tenant.
2. **The creation-time check stays creation-time.** "Child workspace equals project workspace" is enforced when the child is created, not as a standing invariant. The project's own `workspace_id` is immutable in this contract (the same effect as an owner-change refusal today); a future project-transfer child lifts that for one reviewed operation only.
3. **A move is a new fact.** A future design adds a transfer receipt in both workspaces and treats pre-move history as origin history: readable by the destination only through explicit rules, never inheriting the source's approvals, grants, provider connections, domains, guest links or uncertain operations. Those per-resource rules and the destination's billing/quota effect are **unknown** and need their own contract.
4. Until then the supported path is the portable export/import of the WH-077 [export boundary](roadmap/composition-architecture.md#9-export-a-recoverable-application-with-honest-portability-boundaries), with new immutable versions and re-granted provider consent; it is not called a transfer.

## Roles, grants and effect sets

A role never maps to an endpoint's single "danger level". Every registered action declares an **effect set**: the non-empty set of consequence classes its committed effects can have. Authorization is the **conjunction**: the actor's role must allow every class in the set and the actor must hold the grant required by every class in it. An action without a declared set, a role without a mapping for a class, or an effect set smaller than the effects its payload can have, is denied.

| Class | Examples | Owner | Editor | Viewer | Billing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `read` | Project status, versions, previews, saved reviews, approvals queue (read) | ✓ | ✓ in scope | ✓ in scope | workspace plan/usage summary only |
| `private-write` | Draft changes, private specification apply, layout, comparisons | ✓ | ✓ in scope | — | — |
| `limited-exposure` | Expiring, revocable guest preview links | ✓ | ✓ with `creator.guest-feedback` | — | — |
| `public-effect` | Publish, restore live, unpublish, activate a form/module or widen visitor access | ✓ | grant `approve-release` | — | — |
| `provider-effect` | OAuth connect/disconnect, backups, DNS, hosting, connector actions | ✓ | grant `manage-connections` | — | — |
| `spend` | Premium/advanced model approvals, future domain purchases | ✓ (existing exact approval) | grant `approve-spend` (not a default) | — | — |
| `membership` | Invite, change roles/grants/scope, remove | ✓ | grant `manage-members`, limited as below | — | — |
| `ownership` | Add/remove owner, transfer, close workspace, delete project, change payer | ✓ only | — | — | — |
| `billing-read` | Plan, invoice intervals and amounts, usage summary. **Never the payer's hosted invoice link** ([billing](#coordinated-current-schema-consequences)) | ✓ | — | — | ✓ (read-only; owner decision) |
| `export` | Supported project-content export | ✓ | ✓ in scope | — | — |
| `uncertain-readback` | Read-only reconciliation of unknown provider outcomes | ✓ | holder of that class's grant, if still active | — | — |

Rules:

- **No weakest-class bypass, no strongest-class shortcut.** A release that publishes (`public-effect`), dispatches to a provider (`provider-effect`) and reserves premium spend (`spend`) has the effect set `{public-effect, provider-effect, spend}`. Confirming it needs `approve-release` **and** `manage-connections` **and** `approve-spend` (an owner holds all three). Holding `approve-release` alone confirms nothing that also touches a provider or spend.
- **The effect set is computed from the immutable review, not declared by the caller.** The review type declares which effects its payload can carry; the prepared review's digest fixes the *actual* set (destination, provider, spend estimate present or absent). A confirmer lacking any required grant cannot confirm; the review stays pending for a holder.
- **Enforcement is at the sink, not only at the entry.** Each effect sink (publication commit, provider dispatch through `workspaceTransport`, spend reservation, membership change, ownership change) takes the acting context and adds its own class check to its committing batch. An entry whose declared set omits a class its code reaches therefore fails at that sink; the registry test also fails it statically.
- **Or review the effects separately.** An action whose effects belong to different authorities may be split into independently reviewed steps, each with its own class, approver and receipt (for example a publication review and a separate connection review). A combined action never runs under the weaker step's authority.
- **Grants are explicit, owner-assigned, off by default and not transitive.** Four grants only: `approve-release`, `manage-connections`, `approve-spend`, `manage-members`. **Only an owner assigns, changes or revokes a grant.** Nobody can grant more than they hold. No role may self-escalate; a member cannot change their own role or grants.
- **`manage-members` is bounded.** A holder may invite viewers, and editors **without grants**, only for projects inside their own scope, and may remove viewers and editors; they cannot invite or promote to owner or billing, assign grants, change an owner, or widen a scope beyond their own.
- **Billing is read-only (owner decision).** It sees plan, invoice intervals, amounts and usage summary, and **never** the hosted invoice URL (a link to the payer's personal billing details, [above](#coordinated-current-schema-consequences)). Purchasing, payment method, subscription start/cancel and spend approvals stay with the owner. Making billing a purchaser would be a new decision that also needs the real-money gate.
- **Standard AI for members (owner decision).** Editors may use standard assistance within the workspace's fair-use envelope, attributed per actor for abuse review and **zero customer units**; this adds no allowance, seat or price. Any premium or advanced-model spend needs `approve-spend` **and** an exact premium approval; no member request funds premium work implicitly. The owner's answer covered editors only: viewers and billing receive **no model-prepared work** and use direct controls (the conservative default; see [unknowns](#remaining-unknowns)).
- **Resource scope.** A membership covers *all projects* or an *explicit list*; new projects do not join a list automatically; owners always cover everything. Dashboards, usage and history are filtered on the server by the actor's scope ([Chat interactions](chat-interactions.md#shared-authority-and-eligibility)).
- **Chat privacy.** Transcripts and drafts are private to the acting account within a workspace, as are the Chat head and the person's layout preferences and receipts ([dispositions](#source-classified-dispositions-for-the-seven-undecided-entries-and-two-refinements)); model context is built from the actor's authorized projection. Rosters, member emails and invitation tokens never enter model context or Chat history.

## Acting context and admission

- **Tenant admission.** Described in [Converting every workspace-owner path](#converting-every-workspace-owner-path): account-level `admitWorkspace` is unchanged; a tenant admission adds the membership clause to the **same** `identity_admission_checks` insert, so the identity predicate and the membership clause commit or abort together.
- **The clause** requires at the committing instant: workspace `active`; an `active` membership for (workspace, actor) with the revision read at admission; role in the allowed set of **every** class in the effect set; the grant bit for each class that needs one; project inside scope; the ownership epoch unchanged; and for a non-owner role the [collaboration lease](#plan-eligibility-downgrade-and-durable-suspension) current for the membership's epoch **and** `pro.members` inclusion current. Revocation, role change, transfer, suspension or closure between admission and commit aborts the batch.
- **Explicit workspace, never ambient.** Every request names its workspace or resolves it from a project ID whose single tenant is then checked against the actor's membership. Missing or ambiguous is `400`; a project in another workspace is `404`. A cookie or client-side "current workspace" is a hint only. Caches key on (tenant, actor, membership revision) and are discarded on switch.
- **Handler registry and allowlist.** A closed registry maps each converted handler or binding to `{effect set, allowed roles, required grants, feature, operation}`. A non-owner context may reach only registered handlers; a test enumerates routes, Chat actions, bindings and jobs and fails on any unregistered one.
- **Actor-aware feature admission.** `featurePredicate` takes the acting context: inclusion keyed by the workspace, the actor half is the membership clause. For an owner context in a single-owner workspace it must produce the same decisions as today.
- **Provenance rule.** Tenant columns record whose workspace; separate columns record the preparer and confirmer (actor reference, membership ID, revision). A review prepared and confirmed by one member records that actor twice; it is never rewritten to look like the owner.
- **Shared module principals.** The `owner` module principal gains the tenant/actor split through the same context; `visitor`, `staff` and `public` principals are unaffected and never accept a workspace cookie.

## Approvals and release authority

- **Preparer and approver are separate fields.** A member with `private-write` may prepare a consequential review. Confirmation needs the [grants for the review's whole effect set](#roles-grants-and-effect-sets), a **fresh passkey assertion** (the existing ten-minute window) and the existing exact-review binding (immutable version/source/data digest, destination, policy, current revision). The receipt records preparer and approver membership ID and revision.
- **Owner decision (recorded).** A release prepared by an editor must be approved by **another** account who is an owner or an explicitly granted approver (`approve-release`, plus the other grants the effect set needs). **An editor can never approve a review they prepared, even holding every grant.** The rule at commit is: `approver ≠ preparer`, unless the preparer is an **owner**, who retains self-approval (a sole owner behaves exactly as today). If no other eligible approver exists, the editor's review stays pending until an owner acts; it never degrades to self-approval.
- **Reuse, not a new engine.** Approval wraps the existing review types (publication, specification, service client, application, domain, connection, module activation). A shared review-class registry assigns each its effect set so staging promotion and bulk-change work reuse it later.
- **Withdrawal.** Unpublish and rollback are `public-effect` actions; no editor may take a site offline without `approve-release` (conservative; emergency safety withdrawal by the platform is unaffected).
- **Queue.** Pending reviews are visible to eligible approvers through a read binding; opening one never approves it. Existing ten-minute review windows are unchanged and independent of membership.

## Invitations and membership lifecycle

### Invitation contract

A workspace invitation is its own record, never a platform signup invitation. Fields (conceptual): workspace, proposed role, grants and scope (immutable once issued), inviter membership ID and revision, a hash of a 256-bit random secret shown once to the inviter, a masked contact hint, state, expiry. The secret follows the guest-link rules: stored only as a hash, never in Chat, model context, logs, analytics or exports.

- **Existing completed accounts first (owner decision, recorded).** The invitee must already hold a *ready* account. An invitation never creates an account and never changes the deployed signup ceremonies, eligibility, Turnstile or the 50-account quota. In the invite-only test stage the invitee arrives through the ordinary platform invitation first. Invite-as-signup is out of scope; adding it later would be a new identity-workstream decision.
- **Possession is required; an email address alone is never authority.** Acceptance needs (1) possession of the secret, (2) a ready account with a current passkey session (an `enrolling` or `recovering` account cannot claim and its progress is untouched), (3) the inviter's authority still current, (4) current Business Pro inclusion and fewer than five active members, owners included, at the committing instant ([member limit](#member-limit)), (5) the confirmation step below. **Nothing requires the claimant to own a workspace or project first**, and a claim neither creates one nor changes the account's own workspace state ([member-only accounts](#member-only-accounts)).
- **Grants and elevated roles are owner-only.** An invitation that carries **any grant**, the `billing` or `owner` role, or a scope wider than the inviter's own can be **issued and confirmed only by an owner**. A `manage-members` holder may issue only the bounded invitations in the [rules](#roles-grants-and-effect-sets).
- **Claim then confirm.** The recipient claims; the inviter side sees a masked account label and confirms. Confirmation runs in the committing batch and **rechecks everything**: the confirmer's current membership revision, role and grant, the invitation's role/grants/scope still inside the confirmer's authority (an owner always qualifies), workspace `active` and ownership epoch unchanged, `pro.members` inclusion, that the workspace has fewer than five active members at that instant, and that the invitation is unexpired, unrevoked and unclaimed by anyone else. If the original inviter lost authority, the claim waits and any owner may confirm the same immutable content. Owner and grant-carrying confirmations need a fresh passkey. Activation without confirmation exists only for a viewer-only invitation the inviter opted into at issue time; its activation batch performs the same rechecks. First valid claim wins; a second claimant gets the same generic response as an expired or unknown secret.
- **Expiry**: seven days is the proposed routine default and maximum; shorter allowed; no extension. Request IDs make issuance idempotent; pending invitations and issuance rate are bounded.
- **Delivery**: owner-delivered link first (as with guest links). Platform email is a later adapter through the shared job outbox, content-minimal, outside first acceptance.

### Membership lifecycle

States: `invited` → `claimed` → `active` → `suspended` (durable, see [below](#plan-eligibility-downgrade-and-durable-suspension)) → `removed` or `left` (terminal tombstone; the same account can later be invited again as a new membership). Every change increments the membership revision and, where it affects access, the workspace access revision. Unique (workspace, account) among non-terminal memberships; one person holds many memberships.

- **Bound.** The first release permits five active members per workspace, owners included (owner decision, [Member limit](#member-limit)). It is an initial capacity limit, not a pricing commitment.
- **Last owner.** An `active` workspace always has ≥ 1 active owner. Removal, voluntary leave, demotion, personal-account deletion and transfer all enforce this in the committing statements (a guard that sees the preceding statements of the same batch), so two owners demoting each other concurrently leave exactly one. The only zero-owner workspace states are `closing` and `closed-held`, which are platform-continued ([closure](#workspace-closure-and-personal-account-deletion)).
- **Removal and leave** take effect at the next admission; authority is evaluated per request, so no workspace session needs revoking. Effects on the member's reviews, drafts, invitations and connections follow the [invalidation matrix](#invalidation-matrix).
- **Cross-workspace isolation.** Authority never crosses workspaces; IDs from workspace A presented in B are `404`; roster and audit reads are workspace-scoped.
- **Single-owner isolation.** A workspace whose only member is its owner behaves as today.
- **Member-only accounts** are allowed (owner decision, [Member-only accounts](#member-only-accounts)).

### Member limit

**Owner decision (recorded October 8, 2026 UTC): the first release permits five (5) active members per workspace, including owners. It is an initial capacity limit, not a pricing commitment.** It is **planned**: no guard, column, constant or test for it exists at the baseline.

- **What is counted.** Memberships in the `active` state, owners included, in one workspace. A workspace with five owners has no capacity for a sixth member of any role. Memberships in `removed` and `left` (terminal tombstones) are not active and free capacity. `invited`, `claimed` and `suspended` memberships are not `active` and are not counted: "active" in the owner's answer means the `active` state, and nothing else is read into it. Nothing here changes the [last-owner rule](#membership-lifecycle) or who may be invited; it limits only how many may be active at once.
- **Invariant (planned, enforced in the committing statements).** A workspace never has more than five `active` memberships. Every transition into `active` takes the count **in the same batch**, with a guard that also sees the earlier statements of that batch (the method of the last-owner guard), so concurrent acceptances racing for the last slot leave exactly five active members and the loser receives the same generic response as an expired or unknown secret. That covers invitation confirmation, the viewer-only activation the inviter opted into, and owner resume of suspended memberships. A check made earlier (at issue, in the owner roster) is advisory only and never substitutes for the check at activation. The value is held in one reviewed engineering constant, not in plan, price or entitlement data.
- **Engineering choices under the answer (not owner statements; reviewable by the child-1 schema review).** (1) A pending invitation (unclaimed, or claimed but unconfirmed) is not an active membership and **reserves no slot**; issuing, claiming and confirming-pending consume nothing, and the pending-invitation bound that already exists (see the [invitation contract](#invitation-contract)) is unrelated to this limit. (2) Capacity is checked and consumed only at **activation**, atomically, as described above; an invitation may therefore be refused at that moment if the workspace has filled meanwhile. (3) A `suspended` membership is outside the count while suspended; **reactivating it (owner resume) re-checks capacity** like any other transition into `active`, so a resume that would exceed five lists fewer memberships or is refused. (4) The constant lives in one reviewed place and is changed by an ordinary reviewed code change; no process for changing it is specified, and none is needed from the owner. This is not an owner question and this contract does not reopen the answer.
- **Not a seat, allowance or price.** It is an initial capacity and abuse limit. This contract states no public seat count and no price. This contract adds no pricing copy that mentions a limit, and [pricing policy](pricing-and-model-policy.md) still defers seats to WH-009/023/041.
- **Distinct from other bounds.** It is neither the per-account bound on platform-funded workspaces ([question 4](#remaining-product-questions), unapproved) nor a cap on projects.

### Member-only accounts

**Owner decision (recorded October 8, 2026 UTC): a person with a completed account may join and use an invited workspace without first creating their own workspace or project. Member-only accounts are allowed.** It is **planned**; the deployed web welcome gate still requires a first project before workspace access and nothing here has been changed.

- **"Completed account"** is the *ready* identity account of [workspace identity](workspace-identity.md): both passkeys enrolled and the recovery-kit acknowledgment done, as the [invitation contract](#invitation-contract) already required. It does **not** include a first project; the answer is exactly that it need not.
- **Entry.** After a successful claim and confirmation, the account may use that workspace within its role, grants and scope without any personal workspace, project, plan or payer relationship. The first-project welcome is the path by which a person creates their own workspace and applies only then; a member-only account is not routed through it and no workspace, project or owner membership is created for it implicitly.
- **Account-level facts are unchanged.** The account remains an `account`-class row set (identity, profile, welcome onboarding and eligibility) and still counts toward the 50-account platform quota; an invitation never creates it ([invitations](#invitation-contract)). In the invite-only test stage the person still arrives through the ordinary platform invitation first. How the welcome-onboarding row is represented for an account that never creates a project is a schema-child detail ([child packets](#child-packets)), not decided here.
- **Authority still comes only from the membership.** A member-only account has no tenant of its own, so every action goes through the acting context of the invited workspace and its [role matrix](#roles-grants-and-effect-sets). Standard AI for an editor, if any, follows the owner decision above and the invited workspace's fair use; it uses no personal budget or allowance.
- **Losing the last membership** (removal, leave or workspace closure) returns the account to having no membership and no workspace. It is not deleted. What the person then sees follows the existing welcome behavior for an account with no workspace and is confirmed, not redesigned, by the membership child.
- **Creating a first workspace later is not restricted by this contract.** A member-only account that later creates its own *first* workspace follows the ordinary, existing workspace-creation path, including the first-project welcome and the same plan, entitlement and eligibility rules as any other account; this contract adds no extra approval, delay or prohibition for it and treats it as the single-tenant-per-account baseline. Only an account that would own or fund a *second* workspace (creation, transfer or co-owner addition) reaches the unresolved funded-budget bound ([question 4](#remaining-product-questions)).

## Plan eligibility, downgrade and durable suspension

Non-owner access requires current Business Pro inclusion (`pro.members`; `pro.approvals` for approver confirmations) for the *workspace*, evaluated by the existing inclusion predicate. Owner authority does not depend on the plan, but owner `membership`-class actions (invite, role change) need current `pro.members`; exit paths do not.

**Problem.** Inclusion is derived from invoice intervals. If suspension were only "inclusion is false right now", a workspace that lapsed and later bought a new invoice would silently restore every non-owner. The owner's rule is the opposite: **loss is sticky; regain does nothing until an owner explicitly resumes.**

### Three things that must not be conflated

| | What it is | Recorded? | Ends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Current entitlement denial** | The admission clause requires the inclusion predicate to be true at the committing instant. No coverage at that instant means denial. | No. It is a property of the moment. | The instant coverage exists again. |
| **Recorded pause** | A durable `suspended` state with `suspended_from`, which also voids invitations, member-prepared reviews and unsent member jobs. | Yes: lease row, audit event. | **Only** by a sticky owner resume. |
| **Sticky owner resume** | The explicit owner action that re-enables listed memberships under a new epoch. | Yes. | n/a |

**Invariants the design must satisfy** (b319f33 met neither L2 nor L3):

- **L1.** A recorded pause is **never erased automatically**: no renewal, late payment, reprocessed or backfilled invoice, retried webhook, restored backup or later observer run turns `suspended` into `enabled`. Only an owner resume does.
- **L2.** Whether a pause is *recorded* depends on stored facts and elapsed time, **not on whether an observer happened to run** at a particular moment.
- **L3.** An ordinary on-time renewal must not become a recorded pause merely because Whumpo stored it after the period boundary.
- **L4.** The lease only denies. It never admits what the inclusion predicate denies, so current denial is unaffected by it.

### What b319f33 specified, and why it fails

b319f33 let a successor invoice extend the lease if it starts at or before `covered_until`, and let any admission, the hourly pass or a roster read record a pause when it found `covered_until` passed. It also stated that "a missed or late observation delays visibility only". **That sentence is withdrawn.** Two defects, found by an in-memory simulation of the rule:

1. **Normal processing delay becomes a permanent pause (breaks L3).** A paid successor row cannot exist in stored data at the boundary: the invoice must first be created, finalized and paid, and then recorded by Whumpo. Stripe's subscription-invoice documentation ([read October 8, 2026](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/invoices/subscription#subscription-renewal)) says a renewal invoice stays in `draft` for about an hour before Stripe attempts to finalize and pay it. If any admission or hourly pass runs between `covered_until` and the recording, the rule writes `suspended` and the owner must resume after an ordinary renewal.
2. **A late payment can erase an unrecorded lapse (breaks L2).** If a successor is paid late and its recorded service period stays contiguous with the previous one (an **unverified assumption**, see below), its stored `starts_at` is still `≤ covered_until`. When no observer wrote the pause first, the next admission extends the lease and restores every member without any owner action, although they were denied in between. Correctness therefore depended on observer timing in both directions.

Source facts behind this ([`src/stripe-billing.ts`](../src/stripe-billing.ts), `schema/platform.sql`):

- `plan_entitlements` is a bare view over `stripe_paid_invoices`. `starts_at`/`ends_at` come from Stripe `line.period`, and **`created_at` is the Stripe `invoice.created` value (`stripe-billing.ts:83`); there is no Whumpo receipt time**. Invoice rows are immutable, so a receipt time must be a new column at the cutover.
- `stripe_subscriptions` is upserted, so past-due history is overwritten.
- The handler processes only `checkout.session.*`, `invoice.*` and `customer.subscription.*` events (`stripe-billing.ts:160–171`). There is **no refund, dispute or credit-note path**, so coverage cannot be retracted today.
- Preview grants are revocable and issue-time checks require only `endsAt > now` and `startsAt ≤ now + 7 days` ([`src/feature-preview-grants.ts`](../src/feature-preview-grants.ts)); there is no schema check that `starts_at` is not before `created_at`, so **a grant can be backdated**.

Stripe facts used above, from official documentation read on October 8, 2026: a renewal invoice is created, stays `draft` for about an hour, then Stripe attempts to finalize and pay it ([subscription invoices](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/invoices/subscription#subscription-renewal)); automatic retries are configurable, with a documented recommended setting of 8 tries within 2 weeks and a maximum window of 2 months ([automatic collection](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/automatic-collection)); `hosted_invoice_url` is the page that lets customers view and pay an invoice, and `status_transitions.paid_at` exists on the invoice object ([invoice object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/invoices/object)). **Unverified assumptions** (no account, sandbox or event was touched): that a renewal paid after retries carries a service period contiguous with the previous interval; that Whumpo records an on-time renewal roughly an hour after the boundary (actual webhook latency is unmeasured); and what `paid_at` reports for retried, late or out-of-band payments.

### Simulated evidence and its limits

The simulation (SQLite, hours as the time unit, 720-hour periods) ran three invoice shapes against the b319f33 rule and a candidate rule, with the hourly observer running and missed:

| Scenario | b319f33 rule | Candidate rule (settlement window 24 h, a stand-in) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1: on-time renewal recorded 1 h after the boundary (an assumed latency) | Recorded pause whether or not the hourly pass runs, as soon as any admission or pass falls in the gap | Extends; no pause; access still denied during the gap by current denial |
| S2: renewal paid 10 days late with a contiguous period | Observer missed: lease extends and members return without an owner action. Observer ran: pause | Pause in both cases |
| S3: new subscription after a real gap | Pause | Pause |

**Limits.** Three invoice shapes only. The one-hour latency and the 24-hour window are assumptions chosen to exercise the logic, not measurements or proposals. Preview grants, backdating, revocation, refunds, Whumpo downtime, clock skew and multi-invoice backfill were **not simulated**. A passing simulation is not a fixture in the repository and is not live evidence.

### Candidate remedy (not approved product policy)

- **A receipt time.** Add an immutable, server-written `recorded_at` (`unixepoch()`) to `stripe_paid_invoices`. An alternative is the provider's own paid time (`status_transitions.paid_at`), which would not depend on Whumpo's availability but needs its semantics verified. **Which is used is unresolved.**
- **Extension and recording.** A successor extends the lease only if `starts_at ≤ covered_until` **and** `recorded_at ≤ covered_until + W`. The observer records a pause only when `now ≥ covered_until + W` with no qualifying successor, with `suspended_from = covered_until`. Extension is available only while `state = enabled`; once `suspended` is written, L1 applies.
- **W is a settlement tolerance, not access grace.** Access still ends at `covered_until` through current denial. **W has no approved value and its meaning is a product decision**: it is where "ordinary processing delay" ends and "a lapse" begins. A small W makes any payment recovered through Stripe's retries (days to weeks) a recorded pause that needs an owner resume; a large W silently returns members after a gap they were denied through. Neither the simulation's 24 hours nor any other figure is adopted here.
- **What it does not solve.** (1) A Whumpo-side outage or reconciliation delay longer than W records an on-time renewal late and causes a false pause (mitigated only by sizing W against Whumpo's own reconciliation, or by provider time). (2) Payment recovered within W hides the gap from the lease. (3) Refunds, disputes, credit notes, mid-cycle plan changes, clock skew and backfills are unmodeled. The candidate improves the two modeled failures; it is **not shown to solve every timing scenario**.
- **Preview grants as a lease source.** Grants are per (feature, operation, project) while the lease is per workspace. The proposal is that the lease sources are invoices plus workspace-scoped (`project_id IS NULL`) `pro.members` grants, and that a grant is chained from `max(starts_at, created_at)` so a backdated grant cannot bridge a lapse. This is a design hypothesis until a fixture proves it.
- **Transactional revocation.** A revoked grant (and any future refund or dispute) must write `suspended` in the **same statement or batch** that records the revocation (for example an `AFTER UPDATE OF revoked_at` trigger), because a lease with time remaining would otherwise outlive the coverage it was built on and re-admit members if coverage returned.

**Remaining decisions and verification before this section is frozen:** (a) the product meaning and value of W, including whether dunning recovery is continuity; (b) receipt time versus provider time, and Whumpo reconciliation latency; (c) verification of the Stripe assumptions above, by synthetic events in fixtures and, only if the owner authorizes that specific operation, sandbox readback; (d) the lease-source decision for preview grants and a fixture for backdated and revoked grants; (e) the refund/dispute design that first introduces retraction.

### Lease design

**Design: a coverage lease that can only deny.** One `workspace_collaboration` row per workspace holds `state` (`enabled`, `suspended`), a collaboration `epoch`, `covered_until` and a revision. Each non-owner membership records the epoch it was activated or last resumed under.

1. **Effective non-owner access = membership `active` ∧ membership epoch = workspace epoch ∧ `state = enabled` ∧ `unixepoch() < covered_until` ∧ the inclusion predicate currently true.** The lease is an additional condition in the admission clause; it never grants access the inclusion predicate would deny (L4), so a future coverage retraction (see 5) cannot be hidden by a valid lease.
2. **Enable.** The first non-owner membership activation requires current inclusion and creates the row with `epoch = 1` and `covered_until` = end of the contiguous coverage chain containing now.
3. **Extend.** `covered_until` may advance, in a conditional update inside the admitting batch (so ordinary renewals need no operator), only across a qualifying successor under the candidate rule above (`starts_at ≤ covered_until` and the receipt-time test), and only while `state = enabled`. The test is on stored data, not on when the write happens: a renewal after a real gap has `starts_at > covered_until` and cannot extend the lease. The b319f33 form (contiguity only) is withdrawn.
4. **Observe and record.** Any admission that finds a non-owner denied beyond the settlement point, the hourly maintenance pass and every owner roster read run the same idempotent observer that moves `enabled → suspended` with `suspended_from = covered_until` (the lapse time, not the observation time), appends the audit event `collaboration_suspended`, voids pending invitations and member-prepared reviews and stops unsent member jobs. Whether an observer ran no longer changes the outcome (L2); it changes only how soon the voiding happens, and current denial protects access meanwhile.
5. **Revocable coverage.** Revocation of any coverage source writes `suspended` transactionally (above).
6. **Resume is an explicit owner action.** `resumeCollaboration` is a reviewed exact action (owner, fresh passkey, current inclusion required) listing the memberships to resume by ID, revision, role, grants and scope. Commit also requires that the listed memberships plus the workspace's other `active` memberships do not exceed five ([member limit](#member-limit)); otherwise the batch aborts and the owner lists fewer. Commit sets `state = enabled`, increments `epoch`, recomputes `covered_until` from the coverage in force at that moment, and sets each listed membership to the new epoch with a bumped revision. Unlisted memberships stay suspended; invitations and member-prepared reviews voided by the pause are reissued/re-prepared, never revived. Two concurrent resumes: one wins on the workspace revision, the other receives the stored result.
7. **Observability.** The owner roster and `workspace.access@1` show the *derived* truth and keep the three states apart: "access unavailable now: plan coverage ended <time>" (current denial, possibly not yet recorded); "paused: plan coverage ended <time>; resumable" or "not resumable: no current coverage" (recorded pause). A non-owner sees "your access is paused by the workspace" without plan or billing detail. The audit log carries suspension (with lapse time), failed resume attempts, and resume with the listed memberships. Nothing resumes without an `owner` actor in the audit.
8. **No grace.** Grace periods and retention of suspended rows are not approved ([questions](#remaining-product-questions)); until decided, access ends exactly at `covered_until`.

On loss, as before: non-owner reads, edits, approvals, Chat and jobs are denied; content and published sites are untouched; unsent member-prepared reviews cannot be confirmed by non-owners; scheduled work approved by a member stops before dispatch; the owner keeps roster read, removal, export, disconnect and uncertain-effect readback; reconciliation cannot create a new side effect. A former collaborator is never given protected-history access. **Entitlement is not billing authority:** inclusion shows who may use the feature; it grants no spend and no payer change.

| M removed, left or suspended | Void | Fails | Cleared on detection | Void | Void | Revoked or disconnected through the existing path |

## Jobs, receipts and immutable history

- **Jobs.** The envelope's `actor.membership {id, revision}` becomes the **only** workspace actor form (an owner also has a membership); the envelope document and prototype are amended in the same change and a legacy "membership null" shape is rejected. It is admitted only by this resolver. Dispatch and commit re-resolve tenant, membership revision, role, grants, scope, lease and inclusion; a mismatch rejects the stale snapshot. Claims, restored journals and operator re-drive create no authority. Replays and retained history recheck current membership before disclosing a receipt.
- **Receipts and reviews are append-only facts** recording tenant, actor reference, membership ID and revision at the time. A removal changes future authority, never past receipts.
- **Uncertain operations** keep their original authority record. After ownership change or removal, readback is owner-only (or the live grant holder) and a platform obligation completes revocation. Nothing is re-sent because a member left.
- **Rules and automation** do not invent member authority: a rule approved under a membership is paused for public/provider/spend effects if that membership ends, until an owner re-affirms it.

## Audit, lifecycle, protection, export and restore

- **Audit (proposed).** An append-only, content-minimized workspace log: invitation created/claimed/confirmed/expired/revoked, role/grant/scope changes, removal and leave, suspension (with lapse time) and resume, transfer and closure steps, and approvals as references to the underlying review. Each event records workspace, event kind, pseudonymous `actor_ref`, subject reference, membership revision before and after, request ID and time. **No email, token, credential, Chat or content.** Readable by owners and `manage-members` holders; a member sees events about themselves. It survives member removal and personal-account deletion because the actor reference is pseudonymous. Retention is not approved.
- **Lifecycle class.** Fill the reserved `workspace-memberships` class and any new classes in the same change as the tables; run the lifecycle catalogue check. Role-filtered owner views; no model or third-party processor; invitation secrets hashed; classification under [data protection DP-02–05](data-protection.md).
- **Export.** Owner roster export (labels, roles, grants, scope, dates; no emails unless later approved, never tokens). A person's own account export includes their memberships. Workspace export is owner-only.
- **Restore suppression.** A restored backup must not resurrect removed members, claimed invitations, completed transfers, suspensions or a closed workspace: independent tombstones with revisions are checked at restore admission ([data lifecycle LC-08](data-lifecycle.md), [restore admission](restore-admission.md)). A restored receipt confers no authority.

### Workspace closure and personal account deletion

The first draft said the last owner can never be removed or delete their account, yet also refused deletion only for a sole owner with other members. These are reconciled as one rule:

> **A personal account cannot be deleted while it holds any active `owner` membership**, whether or not other members exist. The owner first resolves each such workspace by one of: transfer ([lifecycle](#owner-authority-transfer-lifecycle)), removal or demotion by another owner, or **explicit workspace closure**. Deletion is refused with a clear reason otherwise. A person who is only an editor, viewer or billing member may delete their account; their memberships are tombstoned first, in the same closure transaction.

**Workspace closure** is a distinct, reviewed, resumable operation owned by the workspace, modeled on the account-closure design (exact review, fresh passkey, durable closure row, journaled idempotent steps; [account closure](account-closure.md)):

1. **Review and confirm** (an owner, fresh passkey). The review names the workspace, counts projects, live sites, members, pending invitations, provider connections, domains and unresolved operations, and lists exactly what remains after closure (customer-owned repositories and zones, copies already downloaded, provider-side data, backups until expiry). It states plainly if other members will lose access. A model cannot approve closure; any required prior actions (disconnect domains, remove CMS sites and the like) are listed as blockers, as for account closure.
2. **Freeze.** The workspace becomes `closing`: non-owner access ends first, invitations and open transfers are cancelled, member-prepared jobs void, and ordinary workspace batches abort. Members' personal accounts are untouched.
3. **Withdraw, revoke, hold check, reconcile, purge** in the order and with the semantics of account closure (public serving withdrawn, provider authorizations revoked through the existing paths, unresolved operations reconciled rather than re-sent, then deletion of tenant data). **The purge is an explicit, ordered, journaled sequence, not a cascade:** it deletes the 24 `NO ACTION`/`RESTRICT` tenant tables' rows and the five-table `app_*` foreign-key cycle in an order the schema child derives and tests, each step idempotent and resumable, and removes the `workspaces` row last ([consequences](#coordinated-current-schema-consequences)). The closure row and journal carry no foreign key to the workspace or to any person and remain as the final receipt.
4. **Continuation without an owner.** A `closing` workspace is continued by the platform maintenance pass, not by any person's session, so the initiating owner may delete their personal account once closure has *started* without stranding the tenant. Because workspaces have no foreign key to accounts, there is also no hidden cascade in either direction: deleting an account never deletes a workspace, and closing a workspace never deletes an account.
5. **A sole owner with no other members** does the same two steps in order: close the workspace, then delete the account. A combined account-closure review may present both as one chained flow, but each stage keeps its own durable receipt and either can stop, resume or be refused independently. A sole owner **with** other members has the same two-step path; the review shows the member count and the members are told access ended without any content of the workspace.
6. **Holds.** See below. A held workspace does not purge; it stays `closed-held`.

### Safety holds

Today holds are keyed by `subject_user_id` ([hold triggers](../schema/platform.sql)) with scopes `account` ("everything the subject owns"), `project` and `object` ([content safety](content-safety.md#holds-and-their-scope)): an account-scope hold covers the account cascade, every project of the subject, project owner changes and contact deletion, and customer-facing refusals are generic so a hold is never disclosed ([account closure](account-closure.md) step 2). With a separate tenant that subject is ambiguous, and the feasibility spike showed concretely what goes wrong: a naive transform left an account hold on the owner **no longer protecting the project**, three hold triggers comparing a person with a workspace column, and `safety_preserved_records.subject_user_id` already mixing a project owner (tenant) with `OLD.user_id` (person). The contract therefore fixes the subject model below. **Holds are not activated at this baseline**: no case operation or hold placement on a live database is permitted until the owner accepts the written procedure and counsel completes the WH-133 review ([content safety, activation checklist](content-safety.md#copies-outside-hold-control--required-procedure-before-activation)). Nothing in this contract changes that gate, adds a new one or substitutes for it.

1. **Two hold subject kinds, plus a workspace containment subject.** A hold or case subject is a `workspace` or an `account` (a person). A **workspace hold** preserves tenant data: it blocks workspace purge, closure completion, ownership transfer, project and owner changes, member or owner removal that would change who controls the workspace, and project moves, exactly as `safety_hold_project_owner`/`safety_hold_account_retained` do for the account today. An **account hold** preserves the person's own data (identity, profile, private Chat, memberships and audit references) and blocks personal account deletion and membership tombstone purge. Operator **containments** (`operator_containments.subject_kind IN ('account', 'project')`, joined to projects by `owner_user_id` in `operator_contained_projects`) were not covered by the first draft and need the same split: a `workspace` subject for tenant-wide limits and freezes, `account` for the person's workspace sessions, and `project` unchanged.
2. **Aligned with WH-050, nothing imported.** The WH-050 operator serving stop, session revocation and containment retention work was written on a separate branch while this contract was drafted; it has since merged to main (#186, in the port base `4dea09c`, October 9, 2026 UTC) and is now part of the current schema. It keeps `operator_containments.subject_kind IN ('account', 'project')` and keys session revocation by `account_id`, which agrees with the subject names above; the `workspace` containment subject does not exist until 1b adds it. This contract copies no code from WH-050 and depends on none of it. Because WH-054 lands second, 1b adapts: it adds the `workspace` subject and reconciles the containment view, the new `operator_containment_retained` retention guard (baselined as a cross-type finding in [tenant classification](tenant-classification.md)) and their trigger inputs before schema freeze. Whether an active containment should also refuse an ownership operation is **not decided here**; it is recorded as an input to that reconciliation.
3. **No silent expansion and no escape through the gap.** Holds are placed explicitly per subject by the named safety operator with the existing legal-basis and approver fields. The case surface shows which workspaces a person owns or belongs to so linked workspace holds can be placed in the same reviewed action, but coverage is never inferred. **Automatic fail-closed refusal over related subjects:** any ownership-changing operation (transfer, owner removal, demotion or leave, closure, project move, and deletion of a personal account that holds an owner membership) aborts if an active hold of **either kind** covers any *related* subject: the workspace; any project or object in it; any person whose owner membership the operation ends or changes; and any other workspace in which such a person holds an owner membership. The check runs inside the committing batch. Refusals use the same generic customer response, so a hold is not disclosed to members or owners.
4. **Person-typed guards need an explicit rule.** `safety_cases` carries `CHECK(opened_by <> 'human:' || subject_user_id)` (a self-review guard typed to a person). For workspace subjects the equivalent rule (for example, an operator who is also a member of the subject workspace) is unspecified here and is an input to the same review, not an assumption.
5. **Capture and deletion paths.** New deletion and expiry paths (membership tombstone purge, invitation expiry purge, audit retention, workspace purge) are classified in `tools/safety-hold-inventory.mjs` and blocked under either subject kind; holds extend invisible preservation to anything the new tables hold that a hold could cover.
6. **The subject-model change feeds the existing review.** The taxonomy changes legal-hold semantics. It is recorded as an **input to the WH-133 qualified review and the LC-06 design that already gate activation** ([data lifecycle](data-lifecycle.md)), so that review sees the workspace/account split, the containment subjects and the unspecified items above. No separate pre-freeze counsel or operator approval is added to schema development, this document makes no legal determination, and a blocked verdict from the inventory tool is a review signal, not clearance. The content-risk evidence and reversal tables use a typed `account`/`workspace` subject that mirrors this model ([dispositions](#source-classified-dispositions-for-the-seven-undecided-entries-and-two-refinements)); that typing is a proposal until this review accepts the subject taxonomy.
7. **Provider consent is not transferred by a hold or by any ownership change** (invariant 8). A held transfer does not occur at all; an unheld one revokes the sender-authorized connections.

## Concurrency and recovery catalogue

| Scenario | Required outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Two claims of one invitation | One winner; loser gets the generic invalid response. |

| Approval races approver demotion or grant revocation | Commit aborts; review stays pending for a current approver. |

| Review with spend + provider + public effects, approver holds one grant | Refused; stays pending for a holder of all required grants. |

| Lease expires mid-request | The write's batch aborts on `covered_until`; no partial effect. |
| New invoice arrives after a real gap | `starts_at > covered_until`: lease cannot extend; non-owners stay denied until an owner resumes. |
| On-time renewal recorded after the boundary but within the settlement window (candidate rule) | Lease extends in the admitting batch; no recorded pause; access was denied by current denial during the gap. |
| Contiguous-period payment recorded after the settlement window (candidate rule) | Cannot extend; pause recorded from `covered_until` whether or not any observer ran; owner resume required. |
| A recorded pause exists and a later invoice, retry or backfill arrives | `suspended` is not changed by any automatic path (L1). |
| Whumpo is unable to record an on-time renewal for longer than the settlement window | False pause (a known residual of the candidate); the owner resumes. Not claimed solved. |
| Preview grant revoked, then re-granted before `covered_until` | Revocation wrote `suspended` in the same statement or batch; members stay suspended until resume. |
| Preview grant issued with `startsAt` before its own issue time | Chained only from `max(starts_at, created_at)`; it cannot bridge a recorded lapse (design hypothesis until fixture-tested). |
| Editor who authorized a provider connection deletes their account | Membership tombstoned and the connection revoked first; the stored context uses the stable actor reference, so revocation can still unseal. |
| Two members of one workspace repeat the same module request | Distinct principal hashes (workspace plus actor); no shared replay or receipt. |
| Resume races renewal, removal, or a second resume | One wins on the workspace revision; others receive the stored result or abort. |
| Transfer proposed twice, or accepted twice | One open transfer; replays return the stored result. |
| Transfer completion races removal, plan loss, closure or a hold | Guard rows decide once; the loser changes nothing. Under a hold, refused generically. |
| Workspace closure starts while a transfer or invitation is open | They are cancelled by the freeze step; none completes afterwards. |
| Owner deletes personal account right after starting closure | Allowed once closure has started; platform continues; the tenant is not stranded. |
| Hold placed while closure is `closing` | Closure pauses at the holds step with a generic retention reason; workspace becomes `closed-held`. |
| Lost response on invite, claim, role change, remove, resume, transfer, closure | Authenticated readback by request ID; never auto-resend a first dispatch. |
| Provider revocation after transfer is unknown | Visible pending obligation with retained readback authority; no new write. |
| Plan loss during an unsent member-prepared job | Job stops before dispatch; status stays honest; no maintainer invoked. |

## Remaining product questions

For the parent to put to the owner (not asked here). **No owner answer is recorded for any of them, and none re-opens a recorded decision.** The member-only and member-limit answers closed the former questions on those topics; the remaining questions are renumbered 1 to 4. Where an engineering lean is stated it is a lean, not a decision. Member-limit mechanics (invitations reserving no slot, suspended memberships outside the count until a capacity-checked resume, how the constant changes) are engineering choices in [Member limit](#member-limit) and are deliberately not put to the owner.

1. **Payer on transfer.** Proposed default: the paid interval continues, the recipient subscribes in their own right (sandbox only), the previous payer may cancel at period end, no card moves. Alternatives: require the recipient to subscribe before completion; or I'll specify.
2. **Retention and closure window.** How long suspended membership rows, workspace audit, actor rows, expired invitations and the provider `login` in terminal GitHub revocation receipts (proposal: keep `github_user_id`, redact `login` once the authorizer's account is deleted) are kept, and whether workspace closure has the seven-day cancellation window proposed in LC-01 or none. Aligns with the pending LC decisions.
3. **Late-payment continuity for collaborators.** If a renewal is paid after the period boundary, how late may it be and still keep members without an owner resume, in particular for payment recovered through Stripe's automatic retries (days to weeks)? Options: treat only ordinary processing delay as continuity (engineering lean, window sized from measured latency); also treat dunning recovery as continuity; or I'll specify. No window value is proposed here, and the answer fixes the settlement window in the [lease](#candidate-remedy-not-approved-product-policy).
4. **Bound on platform-funded workspaces per account.** How to prevent an account that owns or funds N workspaces from multiplying the platform-funded budget and project quota. The options below are proposals, not decisions, and **no value is proposed or adopted**: (a) a cap on the workspaces an account may own; (b) counting platform-funded budgets per account, so an account's workspaces share one funded envelope; (c) both; or I'll specify. This is not about a member-only account creating its first workspace, which follows the ordinary creation rules. Until the owner answers, this contract adopts no cap and no temporary limit: it only requires the amplification to be bounded **before multi-workspace activation**, and the unapproved policy is kept separate from the current baseline of one tenant per account.
