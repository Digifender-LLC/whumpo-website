## Verified current constraints

- **21 direct account references use `NO ACTION` or `RESTRICT`**. These include application/CMS authority, domain connections, funded Gateway receipts, redeemed invitations and Chat review presentation receipts. Additional project/version and cyclic review relationships also require ordered lifecycle operations. Raw `DELETE users` is not an implementation.
- `identity_challenges` has optional `account_id` ownership without a foreign key; signup challenges may precede an account. Current account deletion explicitly removes associated challenges, and unclaimed challenges expire after five minutes. `file_cleanup` is keyed only by object key, without tenant linkage. Both need explicit ownership handling instead of assuming cascades cover them.
- Profile/project safety receipts and risk evidence currently cascade with accounts/files; profile replacement can remove its old safety row. Restricted preservation must precede ordinary deletion and exist outside those cascades. A blocked row is not automatically a statutory report/hold.
- Existing visitor deletion can erase current and saved application copies, but its short-lived replay receipts and same-database state are **not** whole-platform restore suppression.
- Current cleanup is selective: contact messages become inaccessible at 30 days and daily cleanup removes expired rows; traffic retains a 30-day UTC window; app usage is pruned by day after 35 days; site-health records after 30 days; removed-project fair-use observations after 90 days. Photo physical cleanup waits one hour and runs a bounded daily pass. OAuth/token cleanup and revocation have separate provider-dependent outcomes. No single account lifecycle currently covers all of these.
- Most site/backend/CMS review snapshots, saved configurations, composition plans, Chat, inspections and immutable finance/safety receipts lack a general retention executor. Logical expiry does not prove physical deletion. The [maintenance inventory](maintenance.md) measures eligible queues, not a deletion guarantee.

## Proposed closure state machine

`review → closing → grace (if approved) → reconciling → primary-erased → backup-expired → complete`, with explicit `needs-operator` and scoped `preservation` work. Persist a stable closure ID and policy version before side effects. Record each resource operation separately so a failed credential revocation does not retain every unrelated photo or block deletion of otherwise eligible personal data.

At confirmation, require fresh owner authentication, current inventory/revision, exact account scope and clear effects. Models may prepare a review but cannot approve. Freeze new Chat/inference/workflow changes, OAuth starts/callbacks, uploads, visitor enrollment/intake and publication/activation. Withdraw sites/apps/CMS/custom-hostname serving through authoritative gates; stopping a UI or revoking a session is insufficient. Prevent late results from creating resources after closure begins.

During a proposed cancellation period, only tightly scoped reauthentication, closure status, permitted exports and cancellation remain available. Cancelled closure does not restore revoked provider credentials, approvals, publication or visitor sessions automatically; each requires current authorization and safety checks. Safety/emergency actions are not delayed or reversed by the ordinary grace period.

Before purge: create independently retained suppression records; distinguish eligible data from exact held scope; reconcile in-flight AI, billing, DNS, backups and resource creation; preserve revocation authority while encrypted tokens and key references remain usable; inventory all owned objects/provider resources and duplicates. A timeout never authorizes another external create/delete or release of unknown spend. Keep unresolved operations separately visible, with an assigned operator and next investigation time.

For ordinary eligible data, erase primary copies, retire any operational payloads that can be minimized, verify object/resource absence, and retain only the minimum receipt. Report primary deletion, external dependencies, inaccessible backup ageing and restricted preservation separately. No “fully deleted” claim while relevant copies or unresolved effects remain. The customer should receive understandable status without sensitive case details or promises Whumpo cannot verify.

## Tombstones and restore suppression

An applied deletion receipt used for immediate retry is not enough. Define a separate durable suppression stream whose lifecycle is independent of the database being restored. A future concrete store choice belongs in WH-019/059; no new database/bucket is provisioned here.

Minimum proposed fields: deletion ID; stable owner/project/visitor or artifact namespace; scope; effective revision/cutoff; created time; policy version; replay-proof reference; completed targets; and outstanding backup/provider acknowledgments. Do not store full emails, prompts, media, source or credentials. Stable IDs/hashes can still be personal data; restrict access and retention accordingly. Object keys needed for cleanup stay in a restricted manifest, not the customer receipt.

Restore must:

1. Keep restored data offline and disconnect all automatic provider jobs/callbacks.
2. Read suppressions and revocations **newer than the backup watermark from the independent store**. Missing/gapped history fails closed.
3. Remove erased accounts, visitors, current/snapshot/staged documents, duplicate operation payloads, media references and applicable R2 bytes. Prevent resurrection by old request IDs, webhook replay, OAuth callbacks or pending jobs.
4. Restore neither revoked credentials/sessions nor obsolete safety approvals. Re-evaluate current policy and exact byte/digest approvals before any content is served or exported.
5. Verify provider/resource reconciliation and destructive-case holds, then record a new restore watermark before allowing supported services back online.

Suppression expiry is allowed only after every permitted source containing the removed data has expired/destroyed or been scrubbed and acknowledged, plus the approved margin. If old backups can be imported forever, suppression cannot have a blind 90-day TTL. Restrict restore eligibility instead of promising both unrestricted old restores and guaranteed permanent erasure.

## Proposed portable account export v1

Prepare through Chat; use a clearly scoped authenticated review for private datasets. The account bundle is a portable archive, not a runnable platform backup or automatic restore authorization. No export endpoint or archive builder is added by WH-009.

- `manifest.json`: `format: "whumpo-account-export-v1"`, export/account IDs, policy/schema versions, creation/cutoff, included scope, per-section revision/watermark, file digests/sizes, explicit omissions and completeness.
- `account.json`, `profile.json`, `preferences.json`: strict customer-field allowlists. Credentials, secret hashes, PKCE/state, vault ciphertext and internal operator fields are never selected.
- `chat/turns.ndjson`: saved turns up to a fixed revision; customer action history is separately allowlisted. Existing Chat export is a useful building block, not a whole-account snapshot.
- `projects/PROJECT_ID/`: brief, cleared site/backend source/configuration/tests, saved full website/service specifications and pinned compiler provenance, component/license provenance, and safe publication configuration. Distinguish private desired services from current activation. Exclude internal proposal/comparison envelopes and authority-only digests; they are duplicate operational copies. Never run exported source. Sanitize archive paths; user filenames cannot escape their directory.
- `media/`: only exact currently cleared owned bytes; recheck suppression/safety at export build and authenticated download. Manifest omissions describe held/unavailable content without including restricted bytes.
- `connections.json` and `usage.json`: customer-visible destinations/receipts, standard zero-credit requests, plans/credits/payment history and test/live status. No provider keys, private finance margins or cross-tenant aggregates.
- Optional **separate** visitor/contact/CMS-content datasets: exact project scope, verified authority, minimal needed fields and current deletion/safety filtering. Platform account ownership does not imply permission to disclose every third party's credential or restricted record.
- `README.txt`: scope, omissions, timestamps, formats, external copies left with providers, and portability limits. If a live dataset changed during streaming, expose per-section consistency instead of claiming a globally atomic snapshot.

Use bounded streaming/chunked preparation, no giant in-memory ZIP, and a final manifest/completion marker whose hashes cover every delivered section. Recheck the authenticated identity and closure permissions during long reads and final download. Interrupted packages stay incomplete and expire; they cannot be relabeled successful. A fixed revision alone does not protect mutable rows deleted during export. Choose a consistent bounded snapshot or document section-specific cutoffs and safely abort when required content is unavailable. Export staging never becomes public R2.

This ordinary portability package is separate from statutory data-access requests, case evidence disclosure and financial record access. Provide a verified request/review channel before making access-rights claims; do not use the bundle's omissions as a blanket legal refusal.

## Provider retention evidence and its limits

The D1, R2 and Workers Logs official pages were rechecked October 1, 2026 UTC. The other references below retain their September 30 review date. These are provider capabilities/terms, not confirmation of Whumpo's configured lifecycle or deletion of any customer record.

- **D1:** Time Travel is automatic for supported databases; Workers Paid can restore up to 30 days, Free up to seven. Restoring does not eliminate older valid restore points. Treat deleted rows as potentially recoverable and enforce suppression before serving. Provider-managed history cannot be treated as selective row erasure merely because an application delete succeeded. [D1 Time Travel](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/reference/time-travel/).
- **R2:** Lifecycle rules are asynchronous; transition timing can lag, especially for existing objects. A rule is not a verified exact-hour erasure promise. Enumerate objects, retention/lock configuration and deletion receipts; no bucket lifecycle rule was changed by this task. [R2 object lifecycles](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/object-lifecycles/).
- **Workers Logs:** Cloudflare documents three-day Free/seven-day Paid retention; exported logs and local copies have independent retention. Request URLs and errors may be identifying even when application logs omit message bodies. Confirm actual destinations/sampling before customer promises. [Workers Logs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/workers-logs/).
- **GitHub:** Removing source locally or rewriting a repository does not remove other people's clones/forks; remote cached/PR references can need separate handling. Whumpo cannot promise recall of customer-directed Git backups/downloads. [GitHub sensitive-data removal](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/removing-sensitive-data-from-a-repository).
- **Inference:** The September 30 WH-020 recheck verified that Go's model-specific privacy table lists Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor as training-enabled and not zero-retention. The current test route cannot support blanket “no training” or “we erase every provider copy” claims and is ineligible for confidential beta content. Local Chat deletion does not undo training use. [OpenCode Go privacy](https://opencode.ai/docs/go/#privacy), [OpenCode Zen exceptions](https://opencode.ai/docs/zen/#privacy). The [benchmark/privacy contract](customer-benchmarks.md) records the exact route distinction; WH-020/021 still require production terms and deletion/retention acceptance.
- **Safety reporting/preservation:** The [current federal reporting/preservation statute](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section2258A) and [2024 REPORT Act](https://www.congress.gov/118/plaws/publ59/PLAW-118publ59.pdf) require a separate qualified applicability/procedure review. Routine 90-day metadata or 30-day backup proposals are not a statutory case schedule. Retention extension, restricted access, disclosure/reporting and authorized release must be decided per legal basis; no automatic deletion of suspected material substitutes for that process.

Remaining qualified review: US privacy/access/deletion duties and applicable visitor locations; roles when Whumpo processes customer visitor data; reporting/preservation and emergency procedures; tax/financial records; provider agreements/data use; copyright/licensing and external copies; published notices and exceptions. This task identifies those decisions without asserting legal compliance or contacting providers/counsel on the owner's behalf.
