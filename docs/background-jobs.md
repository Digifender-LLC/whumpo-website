# ADR: durable background work

**WH-052 — October 2, 2026 (UTC). Engineering decision and local prototype; no runtime dispatcher, new schedule or service enabled.** Reconciles the September 30 ADR (`2dcbb7a`) with current source `e21a106`, WH-071 resource identities and the deployed WH-113/114 actor boundary. The [current job/outbox envelope](job-envelope.md) is the shared contract for subsequent adapters. The selected approach is **D1-owned job state and immutable business receipts, advanced by bounded scheduled passes**. Keep existing hourly/daily maintenance. Implement a separate short-interval dispatcher only when the corresponding domain feature is approved and its measured latency/cost gates pass. Queues is the next delivery option if measured demand requires it; Workflows is deferred.

This decision does not activate scanning, account closure, notifications, customer deployments or a new paid service. [Safety](content-safety.md), [lifecycle policy](data-lifecycle.md), [maintenance](maintenance.md) and exact customer authorization remain authoritative. A job runner can resume authorized work; it cannot supply the missing approval.

Before activating a new dispatcher, measure it against these **initial engineering targets**, not customer promises:

- A candidate one-minute pass selects ten indexed due jobs, caps provider concurrency at two, and stops admitting work after 20 seconds. Each handler has its own deadline. Ten selections/minute is a theoretical 14,400/day ceiling, **not** measured throughput; retries, slow calls, database serialization and fair scheduling reduce it.
- Closure: no long live invocation. Persist one bounded chunk/resource receipt at a time. Grace/backup expiry uses `next_due`; display progress immediately, with ordinary completion governed by approved LC-01–09. The proposed 30-day target is not yet an SLA.

- Backpressure: reserve independent budgets for scanning, closure and notifications; round-robin eligible owners so a busy tenant cannot monopolize a pass. Bound retained rows and admission separately from customer fair-use terms. Saturation keeps safety content held and cleanup visible; it must not drop receipts.

These targets and scheduling changes require connected D1/Worker verification in WH-012/018/050 before activation. A local SQLite transaction rate is not a proxy for that test.

## Options and decision

| Option | Delivery and recovery | Costs / limitations | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Existing D1 plus scheduled passes | Application-owned due index, durable intent/result, conditional lease and reconciliation. Cron is a wake-up; persistent records make missed/duplicate wakes recoverable. | Polling overhead and timer latency; Whumpo must implement fairness, bounded retries, operator backlog and correct D1 atomic transitions. | **Selected initial design.** Reuse the control store and existing operational report; no new service dependency. Keep the current scheduler until feature integration is ready. |
| D1 plus Cloudflare Queues | Delivery can repeat. Queue contains the exact job/delivery identity and envelope digest; handler loads current D1 state and uses the same receipt rules. Transactional outbox handles the gap between committing D1 and sending a message. | Lower polling latency and elastic consumers, but more delivery/retention/backpressure configuration. Lost queue messages/DLQ expiry must not erase work; D1 remains authoritative. | Add only if the connected load test misses the agreed latency/backlog target or measured polling cost is worse. Prepare an exact queue, consumer, DLQ, concurrency and budget review first. |
| D1 plus Cloudflare Workflows | Persisted steps, sleeps and event waits simplify long sequences. Step retries still need external-operation receipts and current authorization. | Additional step/storage charges and another retained state copy. Automatic retries/compensation cannot infer whether a remote mutation happened. | Defer until multiple long, branching workflows make the simpler runner costly or unreliable to maintain. Do not introduce it solely for a seven-day timestamp. |

Queues has at-least-once delivery; use stable application operation IDs rather than assuming each invocation is unique. Explicit per-message acknowledgements avoid replaying successful siblings. Exhausted messages need a configured DLQ; without it, they are discarded. A DLQ still has finite retention and needs an operator/reconciliation path. [Delivery guarantees](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/reference/delivery-guarantees/), [batch/retry behavior](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/batching-retries/), [dead letters](https://developers.cloudflare.com/queues/configuration/dead-letter-queues/).

Workflows defaults retry a step five times with exponential delay and a ten-minute timeout. Configure retries explicitly: do not wrap a non-idempotent remote mutation in automatic retry without checking its durable intent. Persist unknown outcomes and route to reconciliation. Rollback logic likewise needs confirmed effects and fresh scope; cancelling execution cannot undo an email, charge, resource creation or deletion. [Sleeping and retrying](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/build/sleeping-and-retrying/).

## Durable state and transition contract

Runtime integration belongs in the feature packets, not this prototype. The closed version 1 [job/outbox envelope](job-envelope.md) now supplies exact actor/purpose/resource, input/review and transport identities; the fixture validates its structure without granting authority. Use domain receipts already present; never replace a `photo_scan_tickets`, billing, publication, DNS or revocation receipt with a generic `job complete` flag.

1. **Admission:** atomically establish reviewed domain intent and an outbox/job identity. Bind owner, exact subject revision/digest, policy, purpose, destination, required authority and stable operation ID. Reject reuse with different inputs. Keep credentials/raw content out of scheduler records. A lost creation acknowledgement recovers the same identity.
2. **Claim:** conditional update chooses due work and increments a fencing revision with a short lease. Limit claims globally/by owner/class. An expired *pre-dispatch* lease permits another worker; the previous worker cannot advance state using its old revision. All dispatches must first persist an intent guarded by that current revision and cancellation/current-authority checks.
3. **Dispatch boundary:** after intent commits, a crash is ambiguous even if it may have happened before the network send. Persist `awaiting/uncertain` and never automatically acquire it as fresh send work. Lease fencing protects database transitions, **not a remote API**; a late sender can still exist. Provider idempotency must have a verified key lifetime and semantics.
4. **Result:** validate authenticated provider evidence, owner/subject/digest/policy and operation identity. Apply the immutable receipt and domain transition atomically; identical retries read it, conflicting replies fail. A result observed after cancellation remains evidence of the effect but does not authorize subsequent steps or publication. Recheck current safety/preservation independently.
5. **Retry:** retry safe reads and proven pre-send failures with capped exponential backoff/jitter and finite attempt/time budgets. Respect provider Retry-After. A timeout, failed read, expired lease or 404 on an eventually consistent lookup is not proof of absence. To retry a mutation, prove the old sender cannot later act and that no effect occurred, or use a provider's still-valid guaranteed idempotent operation. If neither is available, retain `needs-operator` indefinitely under the approved retention contract.
6. **Cancellation:** stop new claims/steps by authoritative state, invalidate unsent leases and retain in-flight reconciliation. Closure cancellation cannot recover erased data or republish/reconnect automatically. Post-destructive cancellation needs explicit status and fresh authority for any future work. Do not cancel a mandatory legal preservation obligation through a customer job control.
7. **Completion:** a local chunk's data changes, cursor and receipt commit together. External object deletion needs durable intent plus confirmed outcome; the database cursor alone is insufficient. Final closure requires every required phase plus preservation/backup/suppression reconciliation, not simply an empty due queue.

Use separate recovery lanes for due work and uncertain effects. A durable operator/dead-letter record retains reason category, next investigation, operation identity and minimized evidence reference. Re-drive only after exact receipt review with current scope; never bulk-reset `uncertain` to `ready`. Recovery reads must not release holds, grant clearance or regenerate authority merely because scheduler state was restored.

For Queues later: store the D1 outbox in the admission transaction, send IDs, and mark delivery after acknowledgement. Duplicate sends are harmless to the D1 claim. A periodic outbox sweep recovers missed sends. Acknowledge delivery once the result **or durable uncertain/operator state** commits; transport redelivery is not business retry. Older/out-of-order messages are no-ops after validating identity/current state. Poll the D1 operator backlog independently of DLQ retention.

## Trace A: uncertain scan response — conceptual and deferred

1. Keep the photo private/pending. Create a scan job tied to the existing ticket, exact bytes, policy and approved provider. Retrieve bytes only inside the restricted adapter. A durable recovery design for the private ticket capability is required: currently only its hash is stored, so a lost token is not recoverable from the ticket alone. Future encrypted capability storage needs the existing vault/key lifecycle and must not enter queue messages or model context.
2. Claim, persist provider operation intent and then submit. If the provider accepts but the response is lost, keep `uncertain`; no new submission solely because the lease or 15-minute ticket expired. Poll a supported authenticated provider operation reference/idempotency lookup. Never infer a clear verdict from delivery success.
3. An eventual trusted result records the operation receipt. `recordPhotoScan` still checks ticket expiry and current target: a stale ticket/result cannot approve a changed/expired photo. Job completion records that reconciliation finished, not that the photo is publishable.
4. A new scan requires reconciliation of the prior submission, fresh reservation where permitted and current digest/policy. Definitive matched evidence needs the separate restricted case/revocation path; this dispatcher cannot turn it into an ordinary retry. Unqueryable outcomes go to an operator while content stays held.

## Trace B: resumable closure

1. Fresh owner review starts closing/admission freeze and public withdrawal. Store stable closure/version scope; schedule grace end only under the approved policy. Recheck cancellation and restricted preservation before each destructive phase.
2. Establish independent deletion suppression and an exact ownership/resource manifest. Revoke credentials while key access remains; preserve the minimum unresolved-operation authority. Never implicitly delete customer GitHub repositories, Cloudflare zones or purchased domains.
3. Process bounded owner-scoped database chunks using stable keyset/cutoff boundaries. Commit data changes plus cursor/receipt in one D1 transaction. A crash before commit rolls back; a lost acknowledgement after commit resumes from the recorded cursor. Deletes cannot cause OFFSET pagination to skip records. A shared parent remains until dependent phases are reconciled.
4. Treat R2/provider deletions as separate remote effects. Verify scope and current preservation, persist intent, perform the permitted idempotent action, confirm outcome and then advance. A timeout holds that phase; unrelated eligible work may proceed when it does not destroy reconciliation authority.
5. After primary deletion, wait for all relevant backups/exports/provider copies to expire or reconcile under the policy. Report those stages separately. The job journal in the control database is **not** the independent restore suppression store required by WH-019/059.

October 5, 2026 (source and local tests only, not deployed): the WH-018 implementation follows this D1/cron decision. Confirmed closures continue in the existing hourly/daily maintenance pass as the `account_closures` job, reusing the closure's own journaled compare-and-swap steps (the closure revision is the fence; no separate envelope, lease table, queue or schedule). It is bounded to ten closures and 20 seconds per pass, isolates failures per closure and reports health through `maintenance_health` ([account closure](account-closure.md#background-continuation-account_closures-maintenance-job--october-5-2026-utc)).

Planning arithmetic, not measured bills: one pass/minute is 43,200 scheduled invocations in a 30-day month; two indexed due/recovery selection statements/pass means 86,400 statements before per-job work. Statements are **not** billable row counts. Do not scan the whole journal at each tick. Cron has a 15-minute wall limit, and Paid intervals below one hour have a 30-second CPU limit; keep batches/deadlines well inside both. [`waitUntil` is bounded and is not durable scheduling](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/).

For 30,000 small jobs/month, one successful Queue delivery/job is about 90,000 operations; eight Workflow steps/job is 240,000 steps. At one million jobs those become three million operations or eight million steps. If the full included allowances were otherwise unused, those dimensions alone would be $0.80 or $60 in overage respectively. These are different mechanisms, not equivalent complete systems; retries, shared usage, compute, D1, state, external providers and operator time can dominate. A sparse arrival rate favors avoiding new orchestration complexity, while bursty low-latency work may justify Queues even with inexpensive D1 polling.

Job records follow the [proposed lifecycle classes](data-lifecycle.json); runtime integration must extend that single inventory and complete current schema. Use IDs/digests and bounded reason codes, no raw source/media/messages/tokens. Do not prune unresolved mutations, held evidence or restore suppressions with routine terminal jobs. Retain minimal terminal evidence only for its approved purpose/window. Queue expiry and Workflows terminal-state expiry cannot serve as business retention: Workflows documents up to 30 days of completed-state retention on Paid, configurable shorter. [Workflows limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/reference/limits/). Either service adds provider-held metadata that must be included in export/deletion inventory.
