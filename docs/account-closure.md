## Required closure sequence

1. Authenticate a recent owner session and present an explicit, concrete closure review. Establish a durable closure state and stop new work/authorizations/publication before capturing the final inventory. A model cannot approve closure.
2. Resolve restricted preservation and retention requirements before destructive cleanup. An active [WH-015 preservation hold](content-safety.md#restricted-safety-cases-and-preservation-holds--source-foundation-local-only) blocks the account and project cascade, project owner changes and contact deletion or expiry until an audited release, and the inventory reports `activePreservationHolds`. Case records live outside the account cascade. WH-132 extends holds to project and object scope, R2 cleanup (including objects queued shortly before the hold), photo and version removal, visitor erasure, traffic expiry, guest feedback and restore admission. It also adds invisible preservation of profile-photo replacement and Chat draft expiry. `tools/safety-hold-inventory.mjs` classifies every deletion path. A blocked verdict is a review signal, not a legal determination; zero blocked rows is not clearance.
3. Reconcile dispatched/uncertain AI, usage, checkout and provider operations using their existing immutable receipts. Never release an uncertain reservation merely because closure was requested. Document handling of remaining credits before paid plans become available; see [plan-lifecycle.md](plan-lifecycle.md).
4. Withdraw public services and reconcile external domain/CMS resources and pending creation jobs. Database cascades do not remove provider resources. Do not delete customer-owned repositories, zones or other infrastructure by implication; review the exact ownership and cleanup scope.
5. Revoke connected credentials while encrypted tokens and the original encryption key remain available. Resolve in-flight OAuth attempts and sessions so delayed callbacks cannot reconnect the account. Retain unresolved revocation authority until provider confirmation.
6. Inventory and remove eligible private object storage, database content and account records in an idempotent, resumable workflow. Reconcile cleanup queues and orphaned objects. Domain and invitation foreign keys, retained CMS records and other references mean raw `DELETE users` is not a closure implementation.
7. Maintain deletion receipts outside the deleted account's cascade. Reapply them on restores and reconcile external backups and their retention. Define and verify the customer-facing deletion promise before claiming completion.

No new paid services or provider writes are performed by the inventory.

## Slice 1: review, state machine and guards (WH-018 — October 29, 2026 UTC)

Pure source in `src/account-closure/review.ts` and `machine.ts`, in the style of the WH-200 email-change modules. Nothing imports it; it reads no database and calls no provider. The inventory counts and the `docs/data-lifecycle.json` classes are passed in as plain data.

- **Review.** `buildReview(counts, classes)` maps every inventory key to its table, lifecycle class and a disposition: `cancel` (open or in-flight work, revoked or withdrawn first), `delete` (purged with the account) or `retain` (safety evidence, blocked media, preservation holds, redeemed invitations). Each item carries a reason and the class's proposed retention text, or `unapproved`; no window is invented. Zero-count categories are omitted. The digest is a canonical text of key, count and disposition, so a changed inventory changes it. An unknown or missing key, a non-integer count, or a class that does not own the table is refused.
- **State machine.** `requested → reviewed → confirmed → quiescing → closed`, plus `cancelled` (owner, any time before `closed`) and `failed` (system, from `confirmed` or `quiescing`). `closed`, `cancelled` and `failed` are terminal; resuming a failed closure is a later slice. Every event carries an expected revision. Only the owner reviews, confirms or cancels; a `model` or `system` actor is refused. Confirm needs passkey proof within 10 minutes, the digest the owner saw, and an inventory that still matches the review.
- **Guards.** An active preservation hold refuses confirm and close. Open provider work (unresolved AI, OAuth exchanges, uncertain grant revocation, pending credential revocations, unresolved domains, Chat continuations, open connector receipts) and pending billing refuse close only, because they settle while quiescing. The close facts must be observed at or after the quiesce time. Billing has no inventory counter yet, so `pendingBilling` is a caller input.
- **Refusal codes.** `invalid-input`, `invalid-inventory`, `unknown-category`, `unknown-class`, `invalid-lifecycle`, `invalid-transition`, `terminal-state`, `stale-revision`, `owner-required`, `fresh-auth-required`, `review-mismatch`, `preservation-hold`, `provider-work-open`, `billing-pending`, `inventory-stale`.

`node tests/account-closure-contract.mjs` bundles the source with esbuild and checks every edge, guard and refusal code, the agreement of every category with the inventory SQL and the lifecycle classes, purity and non-wiring, and 30 deliberate mutants that must each be caught.

### Schema (created by schema batch 2, October 29, 2026)

The table, the open-closure unique index and the guard triggers now exist in `schema/platform.sql` (class `account-closures`, `tests/schema-batch-2.mjs`); nothing writes them yet. Differences from the proposal below: `account_closures_account` indexes the account, extra CHECKs tie each state to its columns (a digest from `reviewed` on, a quiesce time from `quiescing` on, a close time only when `closed`, a failure code only when `failed`), `account_closure_guard` also requires the history to grow by exactly the new state, `account_closure_new` requires a fresh `requested` row, and `account_closure_retained` refuses every delete because no retention window is approved. The original proposal follows. The table is platform-scoped and must survive the account cascade, so there is no `ON DELETE CASCADE` to `users`:

```sql
CREATE TABLE account_closures(
 closure_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
 account_id TEXT NOT NULL,            -- no foreign key: outlives the account
 state TEXT NOT NULL CHECK(state IN ('requested','reviewed','confirmed','quiescing','closed','cancelled','failed')),
 revision INTEGER NOT NULL CHECK(revision>=1),
 requested_at INTEGER NOT NULL, updated_at INTEGER NOT NULL,
 review_digest TEXT, quiesced_at INTEGER, closed_at INTEGER,
 failure_code TEXT, history TEXT NOT NULL    -- JSON list of states
);
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX account_closures_open ON account_closures(account_id) WHERE state IN ('requested','reviewed','confirmed','quiescing');
```

Triggers would enforce forward-only transitions and terminal immutability, as the source does. Later batches add the stop-new-work flag read by every write path, per-step cleanup receipts, and the deletion tombstones replayed on restore (WH-018 restore acceptance).

## WH-018 runtime: export and resumable closure — October 31, 2026 UTC

Source and local tests only; schema batch 7 (cutover C1). Ported from the closed PR #170 and reviewed as new code: its closure table and state vocabulary were dropped in favour of the schema batch 2 `account_closures` and the slice 1 machine, which decide every transition.

**Routes.** Six same-origin JSON POSTs under `/api/account/v1/` (`export/prepare`, `export/download`, `closure/prepare`, `closure/confirm`, `closure/resume`, `closure/status`), mounted after the exact-Origin gate and before the workspace router (`src/account-lifecycle/http.ts`, registered as family `account-lifecycle` in `docs/boundary-audit.json`). They resolve the ordinary workspace session and run on a database admitted with closure `permit`. No Chat turn, agent action or model reaches them. The owner page is `/workspace/account`, linked from Account & security; it needs identity only, so it works when profile, Chat and the dashboard refuse a closing account.

**Review, then confirm.** `closure/prepare` counts the same inventory the operator CLI counts (`src/account-closure/metrics.json` is the one list; `closure/prepare` runs it as three statements of at most 50 columns because D1 refuses 100) and saves a ten-minute exact review (`account_lifecycle_reviews`) with an opaque digest. Identity rows, funnel events and risk-ledger evidence and corrections are left out of the binding, so a fresh-passkey retry never stales a review. A preservation hold is bound like every other support-only record (decision D72): one placed after the review makes it stale exactly as any other change does. Only an unsettled domain and the other owner actions (below) are listed under "finish these first"; a CMS site, a visitor application, backend review history, backend form settings and every other support-only record get the one generic support sentence instead, and none of them can be confirmed. `closure/confirm` needs a fresh passkey proof, the digest and an unchanged binding; it runs the machine (`review`, `confirm`, `quiesce`) in memory and then commits `requested`, `reviewed`, `confirmed` and `quiescing`, the six journal steps and the first step (`freeze`) in one batch with the identity assertion. A replay of the same review returns the existing closure.

**Stop-new-work.** The fence is one statement: every batch of an admitted workspace database (every read and write of the ordinary workspace router, including a delayed OAuth callback) inserts into `identity_admission_checks` with `denied=2` when `account_closures` holds a `confirmed`, `quiescing` or `failed` row for the account, and the always-empty table aborts the whole batch as `account_closing` (HTTP 409). Only `permit` admission, used by the six lifecycle routes, skips it. The Android API maps the same refusal to `unavailable`. The public site and application hosts stop because the `withdraw` step takes publications offline, revokes guest links and suspends active modules. This is one fence, not a per-route check; a write path that does not use an admitted workspace database (platform maintenance, public form intake against a still-published site before `withdraw` ran) is not covered by it.

**Journal and resume.** `account_closure_steps` holds `freeze`, `withdraw`, `operations`, `credentials`, `holds` and `purge` (operations before credentials: an unconfirmed GitHub backup can only be re-read with its token). Each runs in order, each journal write is a compare-and-swap on the step's attempt count while the closure is `quiescing`, and a step that cannot finish is `waiting` (provider revocation pending, unresolved AI, upload, domain, connector, job or backup work) or `blocked` (a preservation hold or a retained record that needs an operator). Provider revocation reuses the existing GitHub and Cloudflare disconnect paths (`revokeGitHubCustody`, `revokeCloudflareCustody`); keys stay until the provider confirms. An uncertain operation is never released or repeated because closure was requested. `closure/resume` and the hourly and daily `account_closures` maintenance job (bounded to 10 closures and 20 seconds, failures isolated, maintenance health recorded) run the same code; maintenance acts on the durable `quiescing` row alone and creates no session. The purge deletes `users` and moves the closure to `closed` in one batch with the completed journal, behind the machine's close guards (`checkClosable`: facts observed after quiescing, no hold, no open provider work); any hold trigger, foreign key or guard aborts the batch and is journaled as `blocked`. `account_closures` and `account_closure_steps` have no foreign key to `users`, refuse every delete and so survive the cascade as the final receipt (class `account-closures`, content-free counts only).

**Review-time refusals (nothing is taken away first).** Confirmation is refused while any of these holds, so the site, tokens and Chat stay as they are. *Owner actions* (listed with plain wording): an unsettled custom domain (disconnect it); GitHub refresh custody (the owner reviews and confirms the existing app-wide GitHub access removal, which revokes the grant and removes the refresh credential; closure never performs that app-wide action on its own); an unfinished plan checkout (finish it or let it expire; its Stripe session lives one hour, so the count is bounded by that expiry); an active paid plan (cancel it and let the period end, so closure cannot forfeit it). *Support-only conditions*, shown as one generic sentence with no breakdown: a CMS site, a visitor application, retained backend review history or form settings, retained spend records, an uncertain GitHub grant, a lost OAuth exchange, an unfinished key rotation, a Cloudflare DNS action still `sending` or `uncertain` (its outcome at the provider is unknown, and the retention trigger stands aside once the account is deleted, so only this refusal keeps it from being cascaded unseen), a paid invoice record (kept until a finance retention schedule is approved), and an active preservation hold. A preservation hold is one of these conditions and is presented like any other: the same review, the same refusal, the same stale-review answer when it appears later, and the same closure status when it appears after confirmation (the purge is `blocked` with `retained_records`). The `holds` journal step is kept so receipts have six steps, but it is a no-op that is always `done`. A test compares a hold-blocked and a record-blocked closure by deep equality. Record counts in the review cover only records inside their retention window, so an expired record kept solely because of a hold is not shown, and expired lead notes are not exported. A paid invoice record is the one support-only reason shown by name, with a plain statement that support cannot act either until a finance retention schedule is approved (see [owner-review.md](owner-review.md), item 16). Support is reachable while closing: `/api/support/v1` is admitted with `permit`, and the owner page links to it.

**The rule: every condition that can stop a closure after confirmation either has a bounded automatic end or is refused before confirmation.** Bounded ends, all in the inventory windows (`src/account-closure/metrics.json`): an uncertain AI answer, an unanswered form continuation and an unconfirmed GitHub backup after 24 hours, a scan ticket at its expiry, and a job-journal job (an `awaiting`, `uncertain` or held `operator` notification job) after 72 hours of no change. The job window is correct only because every job type today is a content-free email notice whose lost reply has nothing left to settle; a future job type with a real side effect must not inherit it (see the comment on `needsSupportKeys` in `src/account-lifecycle/closure.ts`). A raw account delete with such rows present succeeds, so the purge needs no job handling. A form continuation older than 24 hours is closed as `unavailable` by the `operations` step through the table's own legal transition, because its delete guard otherwise refuses the row. Refused before confirmation: every support-only condition above, an unsettled domain, GitHub refresh custody and an active paid plan.

**Honest status.** Waiting steps are rechecked hourly. A closure still quiescing 96 hours after it began (the 72 hour window plus a day) is reported `overdue` to its owner, whose page then says it is taking longer than expected and links to support, and appears as `account_closures` in the operator report (`node tools/report-maintenance.mjs`, backlog state `attention`). There is no alert: the report is read by an operator.

**Reconciliation authority.** The routes that normally settle in-flight work are behind the fence, so the `operations` step does it: a pending Chat turn is reconciled by `reconcileStandardChat` (released if it never reached the model, marked uncertain if it did) and an unconfirmed GitHub backup is re-read once per pass (read only; no write is repeated). What remains waits, bounded by windows in the inventory itself: an uncertain AI answer after 24 hours can no longer be applied and uses no customer credits, so it stops counting; an unconfirmed GitHub backup likewise after 24 hours; a photo scan ticket once it expires. A lost OAuth exchange has no automated resolver and is a support-only condition. `closure/resume` and the maintenance job run the same step, so "closure finishes even if you do not return" holds for everything except a support-only condition.

**What was reviewed is what is deleted.** The statement that creates the closure also requires the counts the owner saw (projects, live sites, saved versions, photos, Chat messages, records, uploads, guest links) to be unchanged, so a project or photo created between the last read and the batch refuses the confirmation as stale instead of being deleted unseen. The purge batch's repeated settle-first check also covers active subscriptions and paid invoices, so a Stripe row that lands after the last read stops the purge as a retained record rather than being cascade-deleted silently.

**Purge atomicity.** The purge batch is journal swap, closed state, deletes. The swap (the step's attempt count) and a repeat of the settle-first check ride in the same statements as the closed update, so a lost swap or work that appeared since the read closes and deletes nothing, and the journal never marks the purge done while the closure stays open.

**Records that used to trap an account.** `github_grant_revocations` and `domain_connections` (with their claim, reconciliation and DNS-action children) are now `ON DELETE CASCADE` from the account and project: a settled grant review, or a hostname record that is `review` or `disconnected`, leaves with the account and no longer makes it unclosable. The reconciliation receipt (`domain_reconciliations`) stays immutable while the account exists and leaves only with the account's cascade. A DNS action that is `sending` or `uncertain` is refused at confirm (it is not stopped by the trigger once the connection is disconnected and the account is going). An unsettled one still refuses the delete through the triggers `github_grant_account_unresolved`, `domain_connection_account_active` and `domain_connection_project_active`. `github_refresh_custody` stays RESTRICT.

**Not offered.** Owner cancel after confirmation (the machine allows it; withdrawn sites and revoked credentials are not restored, so the UI says there is no cancellation window) and the system `failed` state (a step that cannot proceed waits or blocks and is retried). Tables that reference `users` or `projects` without a cascade and that no inventory key yet names (the remaining CMS review tables, `app_page_reviews`, `app_recovery_points`, `app_restore_reviews`, `app_migration_*`, `app_owner_erasures`, `app_maintenance_chat_links`) are only reachable with a CMS site or visitor application, which are support-only conditions; if one remains after confirmation, the purge batch aborts and the step is journaled `blocked` with `retained_records` rather than deleting anything. The receipt lists what stays outside: the `freeze` and `purge` details carry `githubRepositoriesLeftInPlace` and `cloudflareZonesLeftInPlace` (counts only). Retention windows for the receipt remain unapproved.

**Export.** `export/prepare` binds the account, whether visitor datasets are included and each project's specification, directory and module revisions, and shows exactly what is and is not included; `export/download` needs a fresh passkey proof and an unchanged binding, consumes the review and one of 10 monthly downloads in one transaction, and streams a ZIP (`whumpo-account-export-v1`) that is private, no-store and written with `manifest.json` last. Photos are read one by one only when their exact digest is currently approved, drawing on the account's 5,000 monthly reads before the platform-wide storage ceiling (`account_export_usage`). Never included: passkeys, recovery material, sessions, provider tokens, safety cases, evidence and holds, held or unapproved media, object keys, other accounts' data. The manifest states what it capped: `usage.json` carries the latest 20 model requests, `connections.json` the first 500 GitHub backups and `leads.json` the first 1,000 live lead notes, each with an omission entry and the true count (a separate COUNT). The newest saved website version of each project is delivered in full (`site/index.html`, `site/config.json`); older saved versions stay listed in `project.json` and are counted in the manifest, as are other specification revisions, backend sources and guest preview feedback, none of which is exported (decision D73).
