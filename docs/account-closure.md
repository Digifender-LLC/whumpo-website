## Required closure sequence

1. Authenticate a recent owner session and present an explicit, concrete closure review. Establish a durable closure state and stop new work/authorizations/publication before capturing the final inventory. A model cannot approve closure.
2. Resolve restricted preservation and retention requirements before destructive cleanup. An active [WH-015 preservation hold](content-safety.md#restricted-safety-cases-and-preservation-holds--source-foundation-local-only) blocks the account and project cascade, project owner changes and contact deletion or expiry until an audited release, and the inventory reports `activePreservationHolds`. Case records live outside the account cascade. WH-132 extends holds to project and object scope, R2 cleanup (including objects queued shortly before the hold), photo and version removal, visitor erasure, traffic expiry, guest feedback and restore admission. It also adds invisible preservation of profile-photo replacement and Chat draft expiry. `tools/safety-hold-inventory.mjs` classifies every deletion path. A blocked verdict is a review signal, not a legal determination; zero blocked rows is not clearance.
3. Reconcile dispatched/uncertain AI, usage, checkout and provider operations using their existing immutable receipts. Never release an uncertain reservation merely because closure was requested. Document handling of remaining credits before paid plans become available; see [plan-lifecycle.md](plan-lifecycle.md).
4. Withdraw public services and reconcile external domain/CMS resources and pending creation jobs. Database cascades do not remove provider resources. Do not delete customer-owned repositories, zones or other infrastructure by implication; review the exact ownership and cleanup scope.
5. Revoke connected credentials while encrypted tokens and the original encryption key remain available. Resolve in-flight OAuth attempts and sessions so delayed callbacks cannot reconnect the account. Retain unresolved revocation authority until provider confirmation.
6. Inventory and remove eligible private object storage, database content and account records in an idempotent, resumable workflow. Reconcile cleanup queues and orphaned objects. Domain and invitation foreign keys, retained CMS records and other references mean raw `DELETE users` is not a closure implementation.
7. Maintain deletion receipts outside the deleted account's cascade. Reapply them on restores and reconcile external backups and their retention. Define and verify the customer-facing deletion promise before claiming completion.

No new paid services or provider writes are performed by the inventory.

## WH-018 reviewed account export and resumable closure — October 4, 2026 UTC

**Status: source and disposable local tests only. Not deployed.** `src/account-lifecycle/schema.sql` is a new current-schema input (`tools/current-schema.mjs`), so the automatic `deploy ci` path refuses it; it ships only with an owner-run empty-database cutover ([releases](releases.md)). No provider, Stripe or Cloudflare setting changed, and no live account was closed or exported.

### Routes and authority

`src/account-lifecycle/http.ts` serves same-origin JSON `POST /api/account/v1/{export/prepare, export/download, closure/prepare, closure/confirm, closure/resume, closure/status}` with the ordinary passkey session. It is routed before the workspace router and admits the session with closure `permit`; every other workspace route keeps the default `refuse`. Confirmation and download call `requireFreshWorkspace` (passkey proof within ten minutes); otherwise the browser runs the existing reauthentication ceremony and retries. No Chat turn, agent action or model reaches these routes, nothing is logged and every response is `no-store`.

### Account export (`whumpo-account-export-v1`)

- **Exact review.** `export/prepare` takes a request ID and an explicit `visitorData` choice and stores a ten-minute owner-bound review (`account_lifecycle_reviews`) whose digest binds the account, the scope and every project's current specification, directory and module configuration revisions. Download refuses another owner, a wrong or expired digest, a stale proof and a changed binding (`stale_review`); a reused request ID with a different scope is `request_conflict`. **Export allowance (owner security review, October 6, 2026):** previously each exported photo drew on the platform-wide monthly read ceiling (100,000) and a review could be downloaded repeatedly, so one account could exhaust shared photo capacity. Now a download consumes its review (single use) and one of **10 downloads per account per month** in one transaction, refused as `request_conflict` once used up (the review is kept), and each exported photo first draws on that account's **5,000 photo reads per month** (`account_export_usage`) before the platform ceiling. When the account allowance runs out mid-archive, the stream ends without a manifest (incomplete) and no platform read is charged. `tests/account-lifecycle.mjs` covers single use, both allowances and an unaffected second account.
- **Delivery.** The download is a ZIP streamed from the authenticated platform route. Nothing is staged in R2, no URL grants access, each section is buffered one at a time (test-stage bounds) and every read goes through the admitted database and the scoped private `FILES` binding, so a revoked session stops the stream. `manifest.json` is written last with each file's size and SHA-256, the scope and the omissions; a ZIP without it is incomplete.
- **Composition (existing serializers).** Account/profile allowlist (`readProfile`), approved profile photo (`profilePhoto`), plan and standard-AI usage (`getBilling`), connection status and GitHub backup destinations, the Chat transcript (`exportChatTranscript`), and per project: details, saved version list, the current saved specification, the newest directory snapshot (`exportDirectory`, WH-129), approved owner photos re-hashed after the private read, and staff roles (`exportAccessRoles`, WH-126). Visitor datasets (`exportRecords`/`exportFiles`, WH-127/128) are included only when the review selected `visitorData`; otherwise the manifest lists each such instance as `visitor-dataset-not-requested` (LC-09).
- **Never exported.** Passkeys, sessions, recovery material, provider tokens/ciphertext, object keys, token/secret hashes, safety cases/evidence/holds, held or unapproved media (counted in omissions), internal review envelopes and other tenants' data. Contact inbox messages and visitor application documents are listed omissions with their own existing paths. This is distinct from WH-077 portable project delivery and is not a restore input.

### Closure sequence and journal

1. **Review.** `closure/prepare` uses the inventory counts (`src/account-lifecycle/inventory.ts`, now shared with the operator CLI), lists what is removed, what remains (customer GitHub repositories by name, Cloudflare zones, provider copies, downloads, platform backups up to 30 days) and owner actions that must be finished first: custom domains, CMS sites, visitor applications and backend service/form/activation history (non-cascading references closure cannot remove yet). Preservation holds are not shown. The binding excludes identity and funnel rows so a reauthentication does not invalidate it. **Hold non-disclosure (owner security review, October 6, 2026):** the binding also excludes restricted safety state (`activePreservationHolds`, `riskEvidence`, `riskCorrections`), and every review digest (closure and export) is opaque: a hash of fresh server-side random values with the binding, so equal bindings give unrelated digests. Before this, the digest was an unkeyed SHA-256 of a binding that counted holds, so repeated `closure/prepare` calls (no fresh passkey needed) could reveal that a hold had been placed. A hold placed or released now never changes the review or makes it stale; the holds step pauses the closure with the generic `retention_review` reason instead. Exactness is still enforced server-side by the stored digest and binding. `tests/account-lifecycle.mjs` confirms a review prepared before a hold yields the same client view and still confirms afterwards.
2. **Confirm.** Fresh passkey, the unexpired exact digest, an unchanged binding and no required owner action. The `account_closures` row and all six `account_closure_steps` commit in one batch with the identity assertion; a replay returns the same closure and no second closure can start. There is **no cancellation window** (LC-01 is still an open owner decision).
3. **Stop new work.** From that commit every ordinary workspace batch also carries a closure condition inside its single always-empty `identity_admission_checks` assertion (`denied=2`, abort `account_closing`) while a closure row exists, so read-only batches stay write-free, so Chat, projects, uploads, publication, connection starts and delayed OAuth callbacks abort inside their own transaction (`409 account_closing`). Sign-in, closure status/resume and export remain.
4. **Withdraw.** Publications are cleared, live projects return to draft, guest preview links are revoked and active module instances are suspended (no content row deleted).
5. **Credentials.** The existing `disconnectGitHub`/`disconnectCloudflare` paths cancel authorization attempts, mark every token `revoking` and attempt provider revocation when configured. Tokens and keys stay until the provider confirms; the scheduled connection cleanup keeps retrying. The step waits while any token or refresh custody remains.
6. **Holds.** Any active account, project or object hold stops the closure at `blocked` with only `{reason:"retention_review"}`; resume after the audited release continues. The purge is also refused by the existing hold triggers.
7. **Operations.** Unresolved standard AI, Chat continuations, scan tickets, GitHub grant/backup operations, OAuth exchanges, key-rotation guards and unresolved domain records keep the closure `waiting`; nothing is released or redispatched.
8. **Purge.** Retained finance (`gateway_spend_*`) or GitHub grant-revocation receipts stop it at `blocked` (`retained_records`) for an operator. Otherwise one batch deletes the account's identity challenges and `users` row (cascade) together with the completed journal; any refusal rolls the whole batch back. Deleted photos are queued in `file_cleanup` for the existing private R2 cleanup; R2 absence is not verified by the receipt.

Each step is idempotent and each journal write is a compare-and-swap on the closure revision, so interrupted or concurrent `closure/resume` calls re-run only the first unfinished step. A transient failure leaves the journal unchanged; only a database refusal is journaled. The closure row and journal have no foreign key to `users`, survive the cascade, cannot be deleted and cannot change after completion; they hold counts and states only. After the purge the session no longer exists, so the owner gets the final receipt in that response and later lookups are operator-only.

Re-checking a waiting or blocked closure journals the attempt (revision and attempts increase); a completed receipt is never selected again.

Each re-check of a waiting closure is a handful of D1 reads and one journal batch; no measurement of D1 row charges or contention exists yet. ## WH-019 restore-safe erasure and revocation suppression — October 4, 2026 UTC

**Status: source and disposable local tests only. Not deployed.** The ledger is part of the current-schema input `src/account-lifecycle/schema.sql`, so it ships with the same owner-run empty-database cutover as WH-018 ([releases](releases.md)); `deploy ci` refuses it. No backup was restored, no provider was called and no live data was read.

### Ledger

`restore_suppressions` holds one content-free row per erased or revoked subject: `kind` (`account`, `project`, `visitor`, `staff`, `record`, `file`, `github_token`, `cloudflare_token`, and, for WH-016 abuse reports keyed by the report identifier, `report_contact` for an erased optional reporter email (never the address) and `abuse_report` for a report deleted by retention), the opaque `subject_id` (a staff grant is `instance/visitor/role`), `effect` (`deleted` or `revoked`), the access `revision` that ended a staff grant, and `recorded_at`. No names, values, filenames, object keys, ciphertext or tokens. It has no foreign key, survives every cascade, cannot be deleted and only a staff revision may increase.

Rows are written by `AFTER` triggers inside the erasure or revocation commit itself, so every path populates it, including cascades: account closure purge (account, its projects, photos, visitors, records), project deletion, owner erasure and visitor self-deletion, visitor revocation, staff revocation (owner review or visitor revocation), owner record removal and record expiry, photo removal, and provider tokens moving to `revoking` (disconnect, rotation, grant revocation, expiry) and later being removed once the provider confirms.

### Consulted inside restore commits

- **Logical-resource restores** (re-inserting older rows): `BEFORE INSERT` guards on `users`, `projects`, `app_visitors`, `module_records`, `project_files` and both token vaults abort with `restore_suppressed`; un-revoking a visitor or moving a token out of `revoking` is refused; an older staff grant (insert or update to `granted` with an access revision at or below the revocation) is refused, while a later exact owner review, which carries a newer revision, still grants. Opaque identifiers are random and never reused, so new rows are unaffected.
- **Recovery points:** an app document restore review or confirmation is refused when the point holds a document of a suppressed visitor. Revocation already clears those copies in place; the guard refuses an older restored copy that was not cleared.
- **Specification restores:** a saved version cannot reference a suppressed photo (`version_photos` guard), in addition to the existing foreign key and photo checks.
- **Whole-database restores (restore admission):** a restored backup carries an older ledger, so the authority is the **current** ledger captured from the live source. `src/restore-suppression.ts` (operator-only; no Worker route, Chat control, agent action or model imports it) provides `captureRestoreSuppressions(liveDb)`, `replayRestoreSuppressions(candidateDb, ledger)` and `checkRestoreSuppressions(candidateDb, ledger)`. The replay merges the ledger into the isolated candidate and applies it in one atomic batch (tokens whose revocation was only started become `revoking` so the existing cleanup keeps revoking them with their keys; confirmed ones are removed; staff grants older than the revocation are revoked; revoked visitors are revoked; erased records, visitors, photos, projects and accounts are removed). A duplicate replay changes nothing. `checkRestoreSuppressions` must report `clear` (no suppressed subject present or active and every current entry in the candidate ledger) before restored services may receive traffic. If the candidate's own triggers refuse a statement (an active hold, or a photo still referenced by a saved version whose removal happened after the backup), the whole replay rolls back and the candidate stays held. The existing isolated verifier (`tools/restore-admission.mjs`) is unchanged; the replay and gate are an additional mandatory step before any traffic switch (WH-059), which is not implemented.

### Verification

`node tests/restore-suppression.mjs` runs the actual handlers on disposable SQLite: account closure routes, visitor revocation and self-deletion, staff grant and revocation reviews, owner record removal through the workspace module route, provider disconnect routes and a confirmed revocation, and an app recovery point. It checks the ledger entries and that they are content-free and append-only; that re-inserting each older account, project, photo, visitor, record and token row, un-revoking a visitor or token and replaying an older staff grant are refused inside the commit while a new owner grant works; and, on a SQLite backup taken **before** those actions (as in `tests/restore-admission.mjs`), that the gate reports every kind pending, that a recovery-point restore and a saved-version photo reference are refused with the ledger merged, that the replay reconciles the candidate idempotently, that the gate is then clear and that actual handlers on the candidate refuse the closed account's session and the erased and revoked visitors, keep the revoked visitor's records with the owner and restore only the remaining visitor's document. `--d1` repeats ledger population, in-commit guards and the gate and replay statements on local D1; the backup candidate runs only on SQLite.

### Gaps

- **The ledger lives in the database being restored.** It is outside every deletion cascade, but a disaster that loses the current database loses the newer ledger rows too. `captureRestoreSuppressions` produces a portable, content-free capture, but no job copies it to an independent store; the separate operator restore inventory (`tools/restore-inventory.mjs`, bounded to 512 operator-selected events) is not fed from it. Until that exists, a restore without a surviving current database cannot prove complete suppression (LC-08, WH-059).
- Not covered by the ledger yet: identity sessions and passkeys of surviving accounts (covered by the restore verifier's identity-epoch checks), contact submissions, guest preview links, CMS content, chat history of surviving accounts, saved-version removals (so a photo whose versions were removed after the backup makes the replay fail closed), and R2 object deletion (objects already deleted in the live bucket stay deleted; the candidate's queued cleanup is not re-verified).
- No expiry or compaction: rows are kept indefinitely in this release (LC-08 retention is an open owner decision). The replay is bounded to 100,000 entries.
- Retention promises are unchanged: platform backups can still hold deleted rows until they expire (up to 30 days for D1 Time Travel); suppression applies when such a backup is restored, not by destroying it.
